Describe the comparative shape of the `36` axis pages currently shared by Michelle Falis and Charles Carter.

## Analysis

### Shared Zone

- The overlap between Falis and Carter is axis-only.
- That shared zone is already broad enough to compare method as well as content, because it includes personal, social, philosophical, and aesthetic pairings rather than one narrow topic cluster.

### Difference In Method

- Falis writes in brainstorm clusters. Her axis pages read like rapid associative expansion, with many short possibilities placed side by side.
- Carter writes in longer aspect prose. Even after collapse into canonical axis pages, his material still carries the feel of a classical aspect treatise: tone, conditions, examples, and distinctions between harmonious and inharmonious expression.
- In practice, Falis broadens associative reach while Carter sharpens conditional tone.

### Repeating Convergences

- On [Sun/Moon](../axes/sun-moon.md), both sources circle the problem of inner integration versus inner division.
- On [Mercury/Venus](../axes/mercury-venus.md), both connect the pair with pleasing expression, charm, and attractive communication.
- On [Mars/Jupiter](../axes/mars-jupiter.md), both converge on enthusiasm, confidence, zeal, and action driven by belief.
- On [Venus/Neptune](../axes/venus-neptune.md), both converge on art, refinement, romantic idealization, and the risk of dream replacing discrimination.

### Editorial Consequence

- Falis is useful when the reader needs associative breadth and contemporary scenario language.
- Carter is useful when the reader needs tonal discrimination, especially around how the same pair can express itself differently depending on harmony, strength, and context.
- Keeping both on the same canonical axis page is justified because they often point at the same core zone from very different rhetorical styles.

## Contradictions and Notes

- The main difference is not that one source cancels the other.
- The main difference is that Falis multiplies image-clusters while Carter compresses the pair into a more formal astrological judgment.
