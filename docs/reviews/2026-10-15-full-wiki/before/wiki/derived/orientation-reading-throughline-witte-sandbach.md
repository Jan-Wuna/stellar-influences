## Analysis

### The Stable Reading Rule

- Witte supplies the compressed outcome-key.
- Sandbach supplies the process expansion.
- The comparison only holds when the same orientation is kept fixed, because the activated factor is part of the claim rather than a removable label.

### What Each Source Adds

- Witte tends to state what the formula does in immediate human, relational, or event language.
- Sandbach tends to restate that same formula as an inner movement of energy, awareness, or development.
- Read together, the shared activation page gains both a direct external delineation and a more process-oriented interior reading.

### Why The Orientation Boundary Must Stay Hard

- `Sun/Moon = Venus` is not interchangeable with `Sun/Venus = Moon` or `Moon/Venus = Sun`, even when the same three factors recur.
- The triad hub is therefore navigational only.
- The right comparative unit is `A/B = C` against `A/B = C`, not triad against triad.

### How To Use This During Retrieval

- Start on the activation page when the formula is explicit.
- Take Witte first when the question needs the shortest operational statement of the picture.
- Take Sandbach next when the question benefits from a more reflective explanation of how the same picture unfolds in consciousness or relationship process.
- Keep sibling orientations separate and use the triad hub only to locate them.

## Contradictions and Notes

- The recurring difference is interpretive altitude more than systematic doctrinal conflict.
- This note does not authorize merging sibling orientations of the same triad.

## Derived Synthesis

- The most useful comparative read of shared Witte/Sandbach activation pages is sequential and orientation-locked.
- Witte names the manifested outcome of the picture quickly, while Sandbach opens the same picture into a fuller developmental process.
- In practice, this means the activation page can carry both sources without flattening doctrine: first identify what the picture does, then widen how that same picture is experienced.
