## Source Entries

#### Witte Entry

Reference: [Rules for Planetary Pictures](../sources/alfred-witte-ludwig-rudolph-hermann-lefeldt-rules-for-planetary-pictures.md), one-based PDF p. 309 (entry start).

One’s firmness, perseverance and steadfastness. To be insensible, apathetic, hard hearted. A conservative attitude. Standing on firm ground. Going back to the primary cause of things. To feel paralyzed. A dead soul. The moment (minute) of death.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal firmness and steadfastness, extending to insensibility or immobility.
- psychology: Perseverance, conservatism, apathy, and hard-heartedness.
- body/health: Feeling paralyzed and the moment of death are historical source attributions, not diagnoses or reliable timing claims.
- social/relationship: Hard-heartedness is named without a specific relationship setting.
- events/manifestations: Standing on firm ground and returning to the primary cause of things.
- conflicts/notes: The phrase "a dead soul" belongs to the [Witte entry](#witte-entry); it is not a clinical assessment.

## Contradictions

Steadfastness and apathy are contrasting expressions within the same source. The entry does not explain when firmness becomes insensibility, and there is no second source here for comparison.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Admetos/Admetos = MC`, MC specifies the repeated Admetos pair as one's own firmness, perseverance, and steadfastness. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) joins standing on firm ground and seeking primary causes with a less responsive range: conservatism, apathy, insensibility, or feeling paralyzed. The common thread is remaining fixed, which may be experienced as stability or as an inability to respond. Its language about a "dead soul" and the minute of death remains historical doctrine, not a medical or timing conclusion about a person.
