#### Witte Entry

Reference: [Rules for Planetary Pictures](../sources/alfred-witte-ludwig-rudolph-hermann-lefeldt-rules-for-planetary-pictures.md), one-based PDF p. 310 (entry start).

Buffets of fate. To be tempered. Mighty hindrance. Compression. Forming of blocks or boulders. Hardening. Stoppage. Pressing load. Ballast or heavy weight. Congestion, hindrance. Jamming, compressing, heaviness.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Heavy pressure, compression, and substantial hindrance.
- psychology: Being tempered by "buffets of fate" is named, without a guaranteed capacity to endure them.
- body/health: Congestion and hardening occur without a specific bodily diagnosis.
- social/relationship: No explicit relationship setting is supplied.
- events/manifestations: Blocks, boulders, stoppage, ballast, pressing loads, jamming, and heaviness.
- conflicts/notes: The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes pressure and obstruction, not assured mastery over them.

## Contradictions

Tempering and hindrance may describe different effects of pressure. They are not a demonstrated contradiction, and the single entry supplies no outcome rule.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Admetos/Admetos = Vulcanus`, Vulcanus specifies the repeated Admetos pair through concentrated weight and pressure. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) connects compression and hardening with blocks, boulders, ballast, congestion, and stoppage. Its personal image of being tempered by the buffets of fate belongs to the same range of heavy constraint. The text emphasizes the load and hindrance themselves; it does not establish that a person can withstand, control, or impose them successfully.
