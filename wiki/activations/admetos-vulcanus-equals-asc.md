## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: one-based PDF p. 311, Asc row.

The native looks hard and unyielding to others, or others look that way to him.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 474 (one-based PDF p. 236), Asc row.

Depressive influence through the partner, energy and perseverance of the partner, energy concentration in the surroundings, restriction of violence in the surroundings, violent suppression of others, strengthening of the power of others, deepening of partner influence, and strong environmental influence.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Firmness and concentrated influence encountered through others or the surroundings.
- psychology: The impression of being hard and unyielding can run in either direction.
- body/health: ABC's depressive influence is an attributed association, without a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: A partner's endurance or influence; others' strengthened power or violent suppression.
- events/manifestations: Concentrated energy and restricted violence in the surroundings.
- conflicts/notes: Witte describes mutual perception; ABC extends the range to influence and power, without equating firmness with violence.

## Contradictions

Restraining violence and violently suppressing others are different possibilities within ABC. The entries do not identify the circumstances that distinguish them.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Admetos/Vulcanus = Asc`, Asc specifies the pair through the firmness or pressure experienced in contact with others. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) makes the perception reciprocal: one may look hard and unyielding to others, or see them that way. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds a partner's energy and endurance, deepening influence, and concentrated power in the surroundings. Its range includes both restraining violence and suppressing others, so the sense of firmness alone does not establish how that power is used.
