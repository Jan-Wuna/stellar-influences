## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Discontentment, worries, sorrow through lack and need.

Source reference: PDF p. 306 (one-based), exact entry start, checked on the scan.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Restricted knowledge of the past, success through perseverance and renunciation, consolidation of success through deprivation, criminal freedom restriction, grief through depression experiences, and adverse freedom restriction

Source reference: printed p. 469 (PDF p. 233), exact activation entry. Hades's row on the scan confirms "Depressions-Erfahrungen", correcting "dcpression".

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Lack, deprivation, and restriction, with success also associated with perseverance and renunciation. ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry), [ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
- **Psychology:** Discontent, worry, sorrow, and grief are named. ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry), [ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
- **Body/health:** No bodily diagnosis is given; the source's depression wording remains attributed. ([ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
- **Social/relationship:** Criminal or adverse restriction of freedom is explicit, without a specified intimate relationship. ([ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
- **Events/manifestations:** Need, restricted knowledge of the past, and consolidation of success through deprivation. ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry), [ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
- **Conflicts/notes:** Success through renunciation is a distinct ABC possibility and should not disappear into an entirely sorrowful reading. ([ABC Entry](#abc-entry))

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction is established. Witte names the distress of lack; the ABC entry also allows achievement through enduring deprivation. The passages do not explain when deprivation leads to achievement.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies `Apollon/Admetos` through lack and restriction. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) makes need the reason for discontent, worry, and sorrow, while the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds restricted freedom and knowledge of the past. It also retains a constructive possibility: success achieved or consolidated through perseverance, renunciation, and deprivation. These passages describe hardship and what may be sustained through it, without establishing an earlier state of abundance or pleasure.
