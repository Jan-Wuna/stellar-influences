## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: one-based PDF p. 96, Cupido row.

The parental home. The place of the family. Art located at a special place. Others are envious. Others feel inferior in the family, in a community or in exercising art.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 133 (one-based PDF p. 65), Cupido row.

Blocked community in the surroundings, restriction of a community by others, stable partnership community, consolidated local community, joint consolidation of the surroundings, restricted surroundings for a community, and suppression of a community at a location.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Family, community, or art tied to a particular place, with both consolidation and restrictions.
- Psychology: Witte attributes envy and feelings of inferiority to others in family, community, or artistic settings.
- Body/health: No explicit bodily or health statement in these entries.
- Social/relationship: Parental home, the family's place, stable partnership community, and restriction of a community by others.
- Events/manifestations: Art at a special place, consolidated local community or surroundings, blocked community, and local suppression.
- Conflicts/notes: Blocked, stable, and suppressed restore the scan's distinct meanings; the previous first and conclusion wording was corrupt. Neither entry makes every settled group oppressive.

## Contradictions

The stable or consolidated community and the blocked or suppressed community are different source possibilities. Witte's attributed envy or inferiority feelings are not an explanation for all local or communal restrictions.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Admetos = Cupido`, Cupido specifies the pair through family and communal life anchored to a place: the parental home, the family's location, or art situated at a special place. ABC adds stable partnership and consolidated local community, alongside restriction by others, blocked surroundings, and local suppression. Witte also attributes envy and feelings of inferiority to others within these family, communal, or artistic settings; the entries do not make those feelings the cause of the restrictions. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry), [ABC entry](#abc-entry))
