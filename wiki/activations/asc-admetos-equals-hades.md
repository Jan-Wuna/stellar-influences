## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: one-based PDF p. 96, Hades row.

Disagreeableness at the place of residence. Others are the cause of their own ruin, losses through their own defects or through their inferiority.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 133 (one-based PDF p. 65), Hades row, including its final two clauses omitted by the parser.

Consolidation of past surroundings, consolidation of the surroundings through privations, thorough renunciation of the partner, thorough service to the surroundings, criminal oppression of others, grief through the partner's depressions, and disadvantages through blocked surroundings.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Unpleasant residential or surrounding conditions, with privation, renunciation, and disadvantage involving others.
- Psychology: ABC names grief through a partner's depressions. Witte attributes others' losses to their own defects or inferiority; that judgment remains his claim.
- Body/health: No specific bodily condition or diagnosis is stated.
- Social/relationship: Renunciation of the partner, service to the surroundings, and criminal oppression of others.
- Events/manifestations: Disagreeableness at home, losses or ruin in Witte, and consolidation through privation or disadvantage through blocked surroundings in ABC.
- Conflicts/notes: The full ABC row includes partner-related grief and blocked-surrounding disadvantages. Witte's character judgment does not establish anyone's actual inferiority.

## Contradictions

Consolidation through privation and disadvantage through blockage are different expressions, with no conditions distinguishing them. Witte's explanation of others' losses is an attributed judgment, not independent confirmation of the cause of ABC's hardships.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Admetos = Hades`, Hades specifies the pair through difficulty at the place of residence and disadvantage involving the surroundings or a partner. ABC includes privation, renunciation of the partner, grief through the partner's depressions, and blocked surroundings, while also naming consolidation through privation. Witte describes others' losses in harsh terms of personal defect or inferiority; that causal judgment remains attributed rather than becoming the explanation for every difficulty on the page.
