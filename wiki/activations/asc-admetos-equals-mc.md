## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: one-based PDF p. 96, MC row.

To feel closely connected to the location of the home. The sense of settling down. Others look up to the native and feel inferior. To stick to the given connections.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 132 (one-based PDF p. 65), MC row. The previously attached cross-reference belongs to the following Asc row.

I block the surroundings, I stabilize others, my thorough partner, my endurance for the partner, my restriction by others, my thoroughness for the surroundings, and inner depth of the partner.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Personal attachment to home and established connections, with stabilizing or restricting relations to others and the surroundings.
- Psychology: A sense of settling down; Witte attributes inferiority feelings to others who look up to the native; ABC names the partner's inner depth.
- Body/health: No explicit bodily or health statement in these entries.
- Social/relationship: Keeping given connections, stabilizing others, endurance for a partner, and being restricted by others.
- Events/manifestations: Settling into the home location and blocking the surroundings; a thorough partner and personal thoroughness are also named.
- Conflicts/notes: The full seven-clause MC row replaces seams on top, a damaged translation of the neighboring Asc cross-reference. Others' feelings do not establish actual superiority.

## Contradictions

Personal stabilization of others and restriction by others have different agency. The entries do not reduce those possibilities to a single one-way hierarchy or establish that attachment to place causes isolation.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Admetos = MC`, MC specifies the pair through one's own attachment to the home location and existing connections. The personal part in those ties can involve stabilizing others and enduring for a partner, as well as being restricted by others or blocking the surroundings. Thoroughness and the partner's inner depth preserve a range beyond immobility; Witte's account of others looking up to the native remains a claim about their feelings. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry), [ABC entry](#abc-entry))
