## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: one-based PDF p. 96, Uranus row; the two adjacent symbols are Uranus glyph variants.

Excitements and quarrels with others because they feel inferior. To quit a place for a while. To be absent for a time. Matters of location cause excitement.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 133 (one-based PDF p. 65), Uranus row.

Consolidation of tensions at the location, thorough reform of the surroundings, sudden blockade at the location, sudden suppression of the partner, tensions through restrictions in the surroundings, and surprising resistance of the partner.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Excitement and tension involving location or others, with sudden restrictions, resistance, and temporary absence.
- Psychology: Witte attributes some quarrels to others' inferiority feelings; location itself can cause excitement.
- Body/health: No specific bodily condition or diagnosis is stated.
- Social/relationship: Quarrels, sudden suppression of a partner, and the partner's surprising resistance.
- Events/manifestations: Temporary departure or absence, sudden local blockade, consolidated tensions, and thorough surrounding reform.
- Conflicts/notes: Temporary departure is explicit; escape as the only outcome is not. Reform, suppression, and resistance are distinct claims.

## Contradictions

Reform and restriction both appear, without an assurance that reform removes a blockade. The entries do not establish that every departure is an escape or that every local disturbance has an inferiority motive.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Admetos = Uranus`, Uranus specifies the pair through location-related excitement, quarrels, and a possible temporary departure or absence. ABC makes the tension concrete in sudden local blockades, suppression of a partner, and unexpected partner resistance, while also retaining thorough reform of the surroundings. Witte's inferiority explanation applies to his quarrel statement; it does not explain every local change or make escape the only outcome. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry), [ABC entry](#abc-entry))
