## Source Entries

#### Witte Entry

Others resign, refuse or are unable. The relation to servants and the socially inferior. To come into contact with disagreeable things. Acquaintance with disagreeable people. Difficulties at the close surroundings. Contaminated environment. Ugly or old surroundings. To be in acquaintance with sick, lonely or old persons. Others are in need. Unpleasantness with other people. Formula for a physician (others are sick).

Source reference: Witte, Rudolph & Lefeldt, one-based PDF p. 79, “Asc + Asc,” Hades row; checked on the actual scan.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Contact with need, difficulty, or disagreeable conditions among people and places nearby ([Witte entry](#witte-entry)).
- **Psychology:** Resignation, refusal, inability, and loneliness are attributed to others, not automatically to the subject.
- **Body/health:** The source calls this a physician's formula because others are sick; this historical occupational example is not a diagnosis of the subject.
- **Social/relationship:** Contact with sick, lonely, old, or needy people is explicit; “socially inferior” is the source's historical class language.
- **Events/manifestations:** Contaminated, old, or unpleasant surroundings and difficulties close by are named.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Contact with hardship can include a physician's work; the entry does not establish that such contact causes personal decline. Only one source is present.

## Contradictions

The single entry includes both unpleasant circumstances and contact through medical work. These can describe different roles in the same setting; there is no second source for comparison.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Asc = Hades`, Hades specifies the repeated Asc pair through encounters with people in need and difficult or disagreeable surroundings. The entry includes illness, loneliness, old age, refusal, and inability among others, as well as contaminated or old places. Its physician example gives this contact a possible occupational form: the illness belongs to others in the wording, not necessarily to the subject. These historically framed examples come from one source and do not establish inevitable personal decline. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry))
