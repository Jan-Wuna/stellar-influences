## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

To be married by civil authority. Belonging to a community by order of state. Living quarters provided by the state. Great mansion.

Source reference: Witte, Rudolph & Lefeldt, one-based PDF p. 91, “Asc + Cupido,” Kronos row; checked on the actual scan.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

A special art place, shared direction of the surroundings, hierarchy of a local community, an independent partner community, a state community in the surroundings, precedence of a partner community, an important place for a community, and an important synthesis of the surroundings.

Source reference: printed p. 123 (PDF p. 60), “Aszendent + Cupido,” Kronos glyph row; translation and the line-broken “Partner-Gemeinschaft” were checked on the scan.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Formal authority, hierarchy, or prominence in partnership, community, and housing ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- **Psychology:** Neither entry supplies a personal need for status or formal approval.
- **Body/health:** Neither entry supplies a body or health claim.
- **Social/relationship:** Civil marriage, state-ordered community membership, and an independent or prominent partner community are explicit.
- **Events/manifestations:** State-provided living quarters, a great mansion, and an important community or art place are concrete examples.
- **Conflicts/notes:** State involvement and an independent partner community describe different arrangements; neither establishes the warmth of a relationship.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes state authority and housing; ABC adds hierarchy, shared direction, and independence. These are different forms of community organization, not a claim that the same group is both compelled and independent in the same respect.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Cupido = Kronos`, Kronos specifies the pair through a community or household whose form involves authority, hierarchy, or prominence. Civil marriage, state membership, state-provided quarters, and a great mansion make Witte's examples concrete. ABC adds shared direction, an independent partner community, and places of importance to a group. The organizing form is explicit; the entries leave the emotional quality of belonging unspecified. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry))
