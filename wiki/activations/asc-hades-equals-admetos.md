## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: one-based PDF p. 92, entry start (parser evidence; not visually checked).

Oppressed by disagreeable environment. Places or other people who suffer consistently through want and poverty.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Depression through lack of a partner, restriction of crime in the surroundings, dangerous depression of the partner, thorough disadvantaging of others, manifestation of hidden surroundings, an adverse local blockade, and adverse restriction of the partner.

Translation repaired against the German entry under Aszendent + Hades, printed p. 125, one-based PDF p. 61; wording and Admetos row verified against the scan.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Persistent want and restrictive conditions weigh on people and places.
- **Psychology:** Oppression by surroundings and ABC's historical language of depression are explicit.
- **Body/health:** ABC names a partner's depression; this remains a source claim rather than a diagnosis.
- **Social/relationship:** Lack of a partner and restrictions affecting a partner accompany enduring poverty among others.
- **Events/manifestations:** Local blockades, restrictions, and sustained deprivation are concrete examples; ABC also names restricting local crime.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Restricting crime differs from suffering restriction; the entry does not establish that improvement is impossible.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction appears. Restriction can describe adversity to a partner or place, but ABC also applies it to crime itself. Those different objects of restriction should not be flattened into universal decline.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Hades = Admetos`, Admetos specifies the Asc/Hades axis as hardship that persists or restricts the people sharing a place. Witte describes being oppressed by disagreeable surroundings and others suffering sustained want; ABC adds local blockades, restrictions on a partner, and distress connected with a partner's absence. This supports enduring constraint without implying that change is impossible. ABC's language of depression remains attributed historical doctrine, and its separate example of restricting crime need not describe personal deprivation. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 125.)
