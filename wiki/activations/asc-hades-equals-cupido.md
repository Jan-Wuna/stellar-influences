## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: one-based PDF p. 92, entry start (parser evidence; not visually checked).

To experience disagreeable things through or in communities. To commit adultery, or adultery committed by others. Acquaintances with poor artists or vulgar art.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Disadvantaging other communities, a dangerous community in the surroundings, shared endangerment of the surroundings, joint pollution of the environment, art from the past at a place, and a partner community from the past.

Translation repaired against the German entry under Aszendent + Hades, printed p. 125, one-based PDF p. 61; wording and Cupido row verified against the scan. The former wording about community policy and environmental protection did not represent this passage.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Communal life brings disagreeable experiences, shared hazards, or contact with art and partnerships from the past.
- **Psychology:** Witte describes experiencing disagreeable things through groups without specifying a psychological mechanism.
- **Body/health:** Shared environmental endangerment is named; no distinct illness is stated.
- **Social/relationship:** Witte includes adultery by oneself or others; ABC includes dangerous communities and former partner groups.
- **Events/manifestations:** Disagreeable group experiences, environmental pollution, and encounters with older art are specific examples.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Witte's judgments about art remain attributed; art from the past is not automatically inferior.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction is established. The entries emphasize communal harm in some clauses and older art or partnerships in others; age alone does not make these harmful. The damaged ABC translation has been repaired from the supplied native passage.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Hades = Cupido`, Cupido specifies the Asc/Hades axis through the communities and partnerships in which disagreeable experiences occur. Witte includes group troubles, adultery, and encounters with poor artists or art the source calls vulgar; ABC adds shared environmental hazards and pollution. ABC also preserves a different strand: art and partner communities from the past. That historical association should remain visible without treating everything old, communal, or artistic as corrupt. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 125.)
