## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: one-based PDF p. 92, entry start (parser evidence).

Impoverished female persons, to expose them to danger. Acquaintance with poor old widowed or lonely woman. To become publicly known in a disagreeable manner.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

A woman's old surroundings, disadvantaging women locally, endangered women nearby, dangerous surroundings for a woman, lack of feeling in the partner, disadvantages through other women, a woman's hidden surroundings, and the polluted environment of a people.

Translation repaired against the German Moon row, printed p. 124, one-based PDF p. 61; verified against the scan. “Gefährdete Frauen” means endangered women, not dangerous women; “Gefühls-Mangel des Partners” concerns the partner's lack of feeling, not an absent partner.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** The source places women's vulnerability and adverse public exposure within difficult surroundings.
- **Psychology:** ABC includes a partner's lack of feeling; sympathy or humiliation is not expressly stated.
- **Body/health:** Witte names danger to women without specifying a medical condition.
- **Social/relationship:** Poor, old, widowed, or lonely women are historical Witte examples; these are attributed categories, not universal claims about women.
- **Events/manifestations:** Disagreeable public notice, endangered local women, and polluted surroundings are stated manifestations.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Endangered women must not be recast as dangerous women; that was a translation error.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction appears after repairing the endangered/dangerous mistranslation. The gendered and social classifications belong to the sources and do not establish traits of all women.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Hades = Moon`, Moon specifies the Asc/Hades axis through vulnerable women and adverse public exposure in the sources' historical language. Witte describes impoverished or lonely women, danger to them, and becoming publicly known in a disagreeable way. ABC reinforces local disadvantage and endangered women, while also naming a partner's lack of feeling and polluted surroundings. These are the entries' particular social examples; they do not make poverty, danger, or emotional deficiency inherent in women. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 124.)
