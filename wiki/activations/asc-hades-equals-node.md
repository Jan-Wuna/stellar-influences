## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: one-based PDF p. 92, entry start (parser evidence).

To be entangled. Contacts or relationships to physicians, or sick people.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Relations with the environment of the past, dangerous environmental connections, lack of partner contacts, disadvantages associated with others, connection to hidden environment, and connection with polluted environment

Source locator: printed p. 124, one-based PDF p. 61; Node row verified against the scan.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Connections entangle people with difficult, hidden, polluted, or former surroundings.
- **Psychology:** Entanglement is stated without a claim of inability to leave.
- **Body/health:** Witte names contacts with physicians or sick people, not illness in the person being read.
- **Social/relationship:** Partner contacts may be lacking, while other connections bring disadvantage.
- **Events/manifestations:** Medical contacts and links to dangerous or polluted surroundings are explicit examples.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Medical contact does not itself establish caregiving, chronic trouble, or harmful relationships.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction appears. Lack of partner contacts and entanglement in other connections concern different relationships; they need not describe one uniform social condition.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Hades = Node`, Node specifies the Asc/Hades axis through the connections that involve someone in difficult surroundings. Witte names entanglement and contacts with physicians or sick people; ABC extends the range to links with past, hidden, dangerous, or polluted environments and disadvantage through association. A lack of partner contacts is also possible. The common theme is the setting and consequence of a connection, without establishing a caregiving duty or inability to separate. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 124.)
