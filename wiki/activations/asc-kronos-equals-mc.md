## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source reference: Witte, Rudolph & Lefeldt, one-based PDF p. 94, “Asc + Kronos,” MC row; checked on the actual scan.

To be a workman, employee, official. Inability to occupy an independent position in life. To serve the boss with body and soul. Servility.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

A special minute at a place; my partner is important; my authority at a place; my boss at a place; my self-confidence at a place; my local dominance; the partner's inner greatness; my partner's independence; an important minute at a place.

Translation repaired against the German entry, printed p. 128, one-based PDF p. 63; wording and the mc row verified against the scan.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** One's own relation to authority, work, and a significant partner is foregrounded.
- **Psychology:** Witte names servility; ABC includes self-confidence and dominance, so personal agency varies across the entries.
- **Body/health:** No distinct medical claim appears; Witte's body-and-soul phrase concerns service.
- **Social/relationship:** Employer service and an important or independent partner are contrasting examples.
- **Events/manifestations:** Employee or official roles, one's local boss or authority, and an important local moment are stated.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Witte's inability to hold an independent position cannot be silently reconciled with ABC's self-confidence and independent partner.

## Contradictions

The entries differ materially in agency. Witte stresses inability to hold an independent position and servility, whereas ABC includes one's own authority, self-confidence, dominance, and an independent partner. The page supports that tension, not a single rule of submission.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/Kronos = MC`, MC specifies the Asc/Kronos axis as one's personal place in authority and service. Witte emphasizes employment, inability to occupy an independent position, and serving a boss with body and soul. ABC also names one's boss, but adds one's own authority, self-confidence, and dominance, together with an important and independent partner. The interpretation must retain this difference between subordination and asserted agency rather than make obedience the only outcome. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 128.)
