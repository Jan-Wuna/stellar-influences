Professional activities. To develop or accomplish something with others. One’s activities with others in the near surroundings.

Ensouled work in the surroundings, actions of my partner, my workplace, my work for the partner, my activity at the place, my activity in the surroundings, soul work of the partner, inner work at the place, and inner partner impulses.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Action and accomplishment involving others, with both cooperation and competitive self-assertion represented ([Witte](#witte-entry); [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry)).
- **Psychology:** Desire for appreciation or self-protection appears in Munkasey; Sandbach qualifies effort by patience and freedom from overidentifying with success or failure.
- **Body/health:** Competitive sports and perceived need for self-protection are explicit, without a specific injury or health outcome.
- **Social/relationship:** Family, colleagues, teamwork, partner actions, and work for a partner are named.
- **Events/manifestations:** Professional activity, shared accomplishment, workplace activity, and using competitive sport to seek fame are concrete examples.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Sandbach's spiritual progress can be frustrated by excessive or impulsive effort; Munkasey's perceived threat does not establish that others actually intend harm.

## Contradictions

Cooperative work and competitive or protective assertion are different parts of the represented range. Ebertin's successful teamwork is not a guarantee for every effort; Sandbach explicitly includes excessive work that frustrates spiritual progress, while Munkasey emphasizes recognition and perceived threat.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/MC = Mars`, Mars specifies the pair through action and accomplishment with other people. Professional activity, work for a partner, and successful teamwork connect the person's effort to colleagues, family, or nearby associates. ABC also describes that work in its language of soul or inner life. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry); [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry))

The range includes competitive and protective effort as well as cooperation. Munkasey names fighting for appreciation, responding to perceived threats to one's lifestyle, and competitive sport in pursuit of fame. Sandbach places effort within spiritual growth and warns that impatience, excess, or identification with results can frustrate it. The entries therefore qualify both the purpose and manner of action without making success or conflict inevitable. ([Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [Sandbach](#sandbach-entry))
