## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Personal contact and growing familiarity connect one's relations with self-assessment and development ([Witte](#witte-entry); [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [Sandbach](#sandbach-entry)).
- **Psychology:** Obliging conduct and personal assessment are explicit; Sandbach describes awareness of personality limits and release of old self-concepts.
- **Body/health:** The actual activation entries give no specific body or health claim; the removed Ebertin general and medical remarks were outside coded entry 1117.
- **Social/relationship:** Professional relations becoming personal, new acquaintances, partner connection, and people who help formulate plans are named.
- **Events/manifestations:** Getting to know others and new faces nearby are concrete examples; ABC's local-minute phrase gives no timing method.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Sandbach's spiritual identity development is a distinct emphasis; overdependence on contacts or lack of inner grounding is not stated by these entries.

## Contradictions

Witte and Ebertin emphasize contact and familiarity; Munkasey adds helpful personal assessment and planning. Sandbach focuses on renewing self-concepts within a spiritual framework. These scopes differ without a direct opposition; the general remarks and supplement formerly appended to Ebertin are not independent Node claims.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Asc/MC = Node`, Node specifies the pair through contact that becomes personally meaningful. Professional relations grow familiar, new people enter the surroundings, and encounters can help the person assess progress or formulate plans. ABC adds personal, partner, and local connections as concrete forms of that relational range. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry))

Sandbach gives the development of identity a distinct spiritual meaning: recognizing personality limits, releasing old self-concepts, and allowing identity to reflect present spiritual needs. That claim remains alongside the social examples, without requiring every new contact to transform identity or implying that connection must undermine inner grounding. ([Sandbach](#sandbach-entry))
