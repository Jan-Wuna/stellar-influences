## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

To have to act ideologically in compliance with others. To be with people who are mentally superior.

Source reference: one-based PDF page 98, Ascendant + Poseidon, Kronos row. “Mentally superior” is the source's comparative judgment.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Authority of spiritualized surroundings, distinctive local culture, mental or spiritual authority for others, mental or spiritual dominance in the surroundings, a large cultural place, culture of other states, state culture in the surroundings, an important idea concerning the surroundings, and truth of other states.

Source reference: printed page 137, one-based PDF page 67, Aszendent + Poseidon, Kronos row. The omitted “besondere Orts-Kultur” clause is restored. “Geistige” allows mental or spiritual scope; “Staaten” refers to states or countries.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Intellectual or spiritual authority in one's associations, with ideological compliance in Witte and cultural prominence in ABC.
- **Psychology:** Authority, dominance and the source's judgment of mental superiority.
- **Body/health:** Neither entry supplies a bodily or health delineation.
- **Social/relationship:** Witte describes having to comply with others ideologically; ABC also names authority exercised for others.
- **Events/manifestations:** Distinctive local culture, a large cultural place, and the culture or truth of other states.
- **Conflicts/notes:** The entries give different directions of authority. Ideological compliance is explicit, while dogmatism or manipulative games are not specified.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

`Asc/Poseidon = Kronos` describes intellectual or spiritual authority in one's surroundings. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) places the native in ideological compliance with others and among people it calls mentally superior. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) also permits authority for others and dominance in the surroundings, extending the theme into distinctive local culture, large cultural places and other states. Authority can therefore come from others or be directed toward them; the source's comparative judgments remain attributed rather than objective rankings of people.
