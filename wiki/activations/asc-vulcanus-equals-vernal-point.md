## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Violent and fierce individuals. Others show their superiority.

Source reference: one-based PDF page 97, Ascendant + Vulcanus, Vernal Point row. Superiority and fierceness are the source's descriptions of others.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Energy space of the world, force or violence at the place in public, a public place of power, public force or authority nearby, a place of strength in public, partner influence in public life, and violation of the public surroundings.

Source reference: printed page 134, one-based PDF page 66, Aszendent + Vulkanus, Vernal Point row. “Gewalt” includes force or violence and, in “öffentliche Gewalt,” public authority; no particular institution is named. “Kraft” retains strength, distinct from “Macht” power. “Vergewaltigung” uses the rape/violation term for the public surroundings, without specifying an act; energy space of the world remains undefined.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Others' displayed superiority or violence, with public settings of force, power and influence in ABC.
- **Psychology:** Witte's descriptions of fierce or superior individuals are attributed assessments; no motive or habituation process is stated.
- **Body/health:** Violence is represented, but no particular bodily or health outcome is supplied.
- **Social/relationship:** Others show superiority; ABC names partner influence in public life.
- **Events/manifestations:** Public power or strength places, nearby public force or authority, local public violence and violation of public surroundings.
- **Conflicts/notes:** The broad public-power clauses do not make every exercise of authority violent. Witte's explicit violence remains alongside them, without a stated normalization process.

## Derived Synthesis

`Asc/Vulcanus = Vernal Point` describes displayed power or force encountered through others in public settings. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) explicitly names violent or fierce individuals and others showing superiority. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) develops the public setting through places of power or strength, nearby public force or authority and partner influence in public life, while retaining public violence and violation as possibilities. These support a public manifestation of powerful influence, without assigning a motive or treating every public authority as violent.
