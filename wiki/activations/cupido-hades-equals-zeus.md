## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 267 (one-based PDF numbering).

Poor family with many children. Blessed with many children. The same about nations. To create artistically with scant materials. To create with substitutes.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 425 (printed); PDF page: 211.

Dangerous objectives of a community, clandestine efforts of a community, artistic creations from the past, achievements from a shared past, inadequate achievements of a community, and disadvantages for a community through fire.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: A community's creation, effort, or productivity is expressed under poor conditions or through its past achievements.
- Psychology: No distinct psychological state is specified.
- Body/health: Numerous children are a family example; no medical or fertility diagnosis is established.
- Social/relationship: Poor families with many children, nations, and shared communal efforts are named.
- Events/manifestations: Artistic creation with scant materials or substitutes, old artistic achievements, and disadvantages through fire are listed.
- Conflicts/notes: Productive adaptation and dangerous or inadequate efforts are separate possibilities.

## Contradictions

Witte stresses creation despite scant means; ABC includes past creations and hazardous or inadequate efforts. Their differences do not amount to a direct contradiction.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Cupido/Hades = Zeus`, Zeus specifies the Cupido/Hades pair through collective creation and effort under restricted conditions. [Witte](#witte-entry) joins poor families or nations with many children to artists working with scant materials and substitutes. [ABC](#abc-entry) preserves artistic creations and achievements from a shared past, while also naming dangerous objectives, clandestine or inadequate efforts, and disadvantage through fire. Creation under constraint is the connecting theme; successful adaptation is one expression rather than a guaranteed result.
