## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source reference: printed p. 277 (PDF p. 273).

Union kept intact by mighty harmony and devotion. Art objects of great beauty.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source reference: printed p. 434 (PDF p. 216). English wording checked against the German scan.

Influence of art and beauty, peace with a violent community, shared energy for peace, shared harmonious influence, the strength of shared love, strong influence for peace, and a community's preference for violence.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Communal strength sustains union through harmony, devotion, love, and beauty.
- psychology: Devotion and preference are explicit; a community's preference for violence is also retained.
- body/health: No specific bodily or medical meaning is supplied.
- social/relationship: A union kept intact, shared love, and peace with a violent community.
- events/manifestations: Beautiful art objects, artistic influence, and strong influence for peace.
- conflicts/notes: Peace and a preference for violence are both ABC examples. The entries do not establish hidden power asymmetries.

## Contradictions

The ABC entry includes peace and attraction to violence without specifying their conditions. This internal range should remain alongside the Witte emphasis on harmony rather than be flattened into an exclusively peaceful meaning.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Cupido/Vulcanus = Venus`, Venus specifies communal strength through harmony, devotion, shared love, and art. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes a union held together by harmony and art objects of great beauty. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) extends this to shared peace and making peace with a violent community, but also names a community's preference for violence. Cohesion and beauty organize much of the entry; its final violent possibility prevents treating communal harmony as unconditional.
