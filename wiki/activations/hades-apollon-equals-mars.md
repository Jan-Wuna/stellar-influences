## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Ugly acts. Trade or professional activity in connection with old material. Antiquity or historical scientific activity. Active in occult sciences or magic. Activity connected with historical sciences.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Editing old knowledge, success in work through deprivation, successful work service, secret trade activity, criminal science activity, lack of freedom of will, and hidden extension of activity

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Work and action engage old material, historical knowledge, or concealed activity.
- **Psychology:** The ABC entry mentions constrained freedom of will; neither entry establishes that work with difficult material changes a person's character.
- **Body/health:** No distinct bodily or medical claim is made in these entries.
- **Social/relationship:** Trade, professional service, and secret commercial activity provide the social settings.
- **Events/manifestations:** Historical research, work with antiquities, occult practice, and activity described by the source as ugly or criminal are included.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Legitimate scholarship and harmful activity are alternatives in the evidence, not moral equivalents or inevitable outcomes of one another.

## Contradictions

The entries emphasize different kinds of work without establishing a direct contradiction. Their negative alternatives do not establish moral coarsening through work with old material.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Apollon = Mars`, Mars gives the pair an active or occupational expression: handling old material and applying historical or concealed knowledge. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) places this in trade, historical science, antiquities, and occult work, while also retaining “ugly acts” as a separate adverse possibility. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds work through deprivation, service, secret trade, and criminal scientific activity (printed p. 442).

The supported throughline is the work being done and its difficult or hidden subject matter. These entries do not show that scholarly, commercial, and criminal manifestations are interchangeable, or that any one of them must occur.
