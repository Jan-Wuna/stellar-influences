## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: printed p. 284; PDF p. 280.

Great impoverishment of the people, extensive mass suffering. Epidemics. Lack of water. Drought and hot periods.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: printed p. 442; PDF p. 220, Moon row.

German: "Erfolg eines Volkes durch Entbehrungen, gefährdete Freiheit einer Frau, gefährliche Erfahrungen mit einer Frau, Kummer-Vermehrung im Volk, Mangel an Wissen über eine Frau, Verbreitung von Gemüts-Kummer, vergangener Erfolg einer Frau, Wissen einer alten Frau".

English translation: Success of a people through privation; endangered freedom of a woman; dangerous experiences with a woman; increased sorrow among a people; lack of knowledge about a woman; spread of emotional sorrow; past success of a woman; knowledge of an old woman.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** The pair's hardship is expressed among a population, with grief and deprivation prominent.
- **Psychology:** The ABC entry names spreading grief; it does not establish that suffering becomes a person's permanent emotional state.
- **Body/health:** Witte lists epidemics and shortages of water as collective conditions, not individual medical predictions.
- **Social/relationship:** Mass impoverishment and suffering concern a people. ABC also preserves source-specific examples involving women, which are not universal claims about women.
- **Events/manifestations:** Drought, hot periods, water shortage, epidemics, deprivation, and knowledge held by an older woman are represented.
- **Conflicts/notes:** The entries combine adverse conditions with success through privation and knowledge. Gemüts-Kummer means emotional sorrow; gefährdete Freiheit is endangered freedom.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction is established. The source's gendered examples do not establish universal female suffering. Success through privation and a woman's past success remain distinct from the listed adverse conditions.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Apollon = Moon`, Moon places the pair's hardship in the life of a population. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) gives concrete collective settings: impoverishment, mass suffering, epidemics, and water shortage during drought or heat. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds spreading emotional sorrow and communal grief, success through privation, a woman's endangered freedom or past success, and an older woman's knowledge.

The clearest connection is hardship experienced by people, together with knowledge or achievement arising in that setting. The historical health and gender examples remain attributed possibilities; they do not establish an inevitable illness, a fixed emotional climate, or a universal statement about women.
