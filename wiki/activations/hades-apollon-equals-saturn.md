## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: printed p. 284; PDF p. 280.

Beginning or ending of general misery of the masses. Misery of refugees. Inflation, impoverishment. General desperation, bankruptcies, suicides.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: printed p. 443; PDF p. 220, Saturn row.

German: "Erfolg durch Härten und Entbehrungen, Konzentration auf Wissen der Vergangenheit, Mangel an ernstem Wissen, problematisches Geheim-Wissen, viel Kummer und Schmerzen, verborgene Handels-Verluste, viel Dienen und Dulden".

English translation: Success through hardships and privations; concentration on knowledge of the past; lack of serious knowledge; problematic secret knowledge; much sorrow and pain; hidden trade losses; much serving and enduring.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Collective hardship has limits, losses, and periods of beginning or ending.
- **Psychology:** Desperation and sorrow are source themes, not inevitable mental states of a person with the formula.
- **Body/health:** ABC names pain; Witte's historical list includes suicides. Neither establishes a diagnosis or an individual outcome.
- **Social/relationship:** Refugees, mass impoverishment, and trade losses locate hardship in collective conditions.
- **Events/manifestations:** Inflation, bankruptcy, refugee suffering, concentrated historical study, hidden trade losses, and much serving and enduring are represented.
- **Conflicts/notes:** Witte expressly allows an ending as well as a beginning of misery. ABC also retains success through hardship, so change and achievement remain represented alongside loss.

## Contradictions

The entries do not establish a direct contradiction. Success through hardships coexists with sorrow, pain and loss; Witte explicitly includes the beginning or ending of mass misery.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Apollon = Saturn`, Saturn specifies the pair through hardship, loss, and its boundaries. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) explicitly includes both the beginning and the ending of mass misery, then lists refugee suffering, inflation, impoverishment, and bankruptcy among its historical examples (PDF entry start p. 280). The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds concentration on past knowledge, lack of serious knowledge, problematic secret knowledge, concealed trade losses, much serving and enduring, and success through hardships and privation. The formula therefore does not warrant a claim of permanent or inevitable despair; the severe psychological outcomes in the source remain attributed possibilities.
