## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: PDF p. 279.

Atrocities. Great mortality. Sacrifices of human lives. Great crimes of or against the government. Failure of a plot.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: printed p. 440; PDF p. 219, Vernal Point row.

reputation in public life from the past, lack of authority in public, special guided criminality in public, disadvantages in public, public state service, and state secrets in public

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Adverse authority in public life, alongside public service and past standing ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry), [ABC Entry](#abc-entry)).
- Psychology: No individual psychological profile is described.
- Body/health: High mortality is a historical collective event claim, not a prediction.
- Social/relationship: Crimes by or against government and public state service are distinct roles.
- Events/manifestations: Atrocities, human sacrifice, failure of a plot, state secrets and public disadvantages are named.
- Conflicts/notes: The direction by or against government is retained; failed plots and service broaden the account beyond catastrophe.

## Contradictions

Witte lists extreme public events and a plot's failure; ABC adds public service, past standing and authority deficits. These different public forms are not equivalent to universal institutional criminality.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Kronos = Vernal Point`, Vernal Point specifies adverse authority at a public level. Crimes by or against government, atrocities, high mortality and a plot's failure are Witte's severe collective examples ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry)). ABC extends the public setting to authority deficits, disadvantages, directed crime and state secrets, while also retaining public state service and standing in public life from the past ([ABC Entry](#abc-entry)).
