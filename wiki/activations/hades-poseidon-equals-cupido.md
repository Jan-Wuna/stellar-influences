## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

To resent the masses. To be happy in special or exclusive circles. To be an adherent of odd ideological groups which are considered as fractional groups.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Community in old age, cultural community in danger, common cultural past, common spiritual service, lack of clarity of a community, hidden truth of a community, and renunciation of a spiritual community

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Mental or cultural belonging in a selective community ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry); [ABC Entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 449).
- Psychology: Witte contrasts resentment of the masses with happiness in special circles.
- Body/health: Neither entry establishes a specific health condition; old age is a community setting in ABC.
- Social/relationship: Exclusive ideological groups, a shared cultural past, and common spiritual service are explicit.
- Events/manifestations: Community participation may involve unclear aims, hidden truths, danger to culture, or renunciation.
- Conflicts/notes: Witte's description of groups as odd or fractional is the source's evaluation, not a universal judgment. Witte's parser locator is one-based PDF p. 286 (entry start), not a verified printed-page reference.

## Contradictions

Happiness within a special circle and risks or lack of clarity within a community are distinct possibilities. Neither source establishes that all such communities are hostile to wider society.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Poseidon = Cupido`, Cupido specifies the Hades/Poseidon pair through a community of thought or culture. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) describes comfort in special circles and adherence to groups it calls unusual or fractional. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 449) extends the field to a shared cultural past and spiritual service, while retaining unclear communal understanding and cultural danger. Selective belonging is the connecting theme; eccentricity or resentment is one source's particular expression of it.
