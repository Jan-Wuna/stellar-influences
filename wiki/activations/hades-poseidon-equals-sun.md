## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. 286, Hades + Poseidon, oriented activator row.

Odd person, crank. Conservative thinking, to stick to tradition.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 448; one-based PDF p. 223, Hades + Poseidon, Sun row.

Mystery of mind and body, a man's intellectual or spiritual distress, an endangered cultural center, lack of human reason, deficient bodily culture, a hidden cultural center, a past cultural center, and a person's reasonable renunciation.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: A person marked by traditional thinking or an unusual mental position ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry)).
- Psychology: Witte names conservative thinking; ABC adds mental distress without establishing its cause.
- Body/health: ABC's mind/body and bodily-culture terms do not establish a specific diagnosis.
- Social/relationship: ABC's reference to a man's distress remains a historical gender association.
- Events/manifestations: Hidden, past, or endangered cultural centers are ABC's institutional examples (printed p. 448).
- Conflicts/notes: ABC gives lack of human reason rather than courage, and reasonable renunciation by a person. Its bodily-culture and distress phrases remain historical source language without a specified diagnosis.

## Contradictions

Traditional thinking and distress are different descriptions. Neither source establishes originality as a necessary benefit of standing apart.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Poseidon = Sun`, Sun specifies the Hades/Poseidon pair through the person who embodies its mental position. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) describes adherence to tradition and uses the evaluative label of an odd person. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 448) adds mental distress and mind/body concerns, alongside hidden or past cultural centers. This supports traditional or unusual thinking as a personal condition, without proving creative originality or a medical disorder.
