## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. 286, Hades + Poseidon, oriented activator row.

Mental blindness and poverty in general. To deal generally with people where no mental contact can be made.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 448; one-based PDF p. 223, Hades + Poseidon, Vernal Point row.

An old spirit in public, endangerment of public culture, intellectual or spiritual service in public life, intellectual or spiritual distress in public, the cultural past in public, and lack of clarity in public life.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Difficulty establishing mental contact in a general or public setting ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry)).
- Psychology: Witte's `mental blindness and poverty` is an attributed judgment, not a psychological assessment of the public.
- Body/health: No specific bodily condition is stated; the word blindness is used in a mental phrase.
- Social/relationship: The public sphere and general dealings with people are explicit.
- Events/manifestations: ABC gives an older spirit and cultural past in public life, endangered public culture, service, distress and lack of clarity.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC's complete six-clause public row includes service, distress, cultural endangerment and lack of clarity. These examples extend the public setting without making Witte's negative judgment universal.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes difficulty making mental contact in general dealings. ABC adds public cultural history, service, distress and lack of clarity; their overlap is the public setting rather than a single judgment about all public life.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Poseidon = Vernal Point`, Vernal Point specifies the Hades/Poseidon pair through general dealings and public life. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) describes encounters in which mental contact cannot be made. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) adds an older spirit and cultural past in public, endangered public culture, intellectual or spiritual service, distress and lack of clarity. These are distinct public expressions rather than a single assessment of an entire society.
