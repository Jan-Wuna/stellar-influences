## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Gigantic crimes and evil deeds or meanness. Heavy oppression and suppression by governments and ruling powers.

Source: PDF p. 285, Kronos entry under Hades/Vulcanus.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

English translation: Special lack of energy, dangerous influence of authority, secret superiority of power, criminal violence in the state, lack of state power, deficient state influence, hidden power of the hierarchy, and the state's renunciation of force or violence.

Source: printed p. 447 (PDF p. 222), Kronos entry under Hades/Vulcanus.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Harmful force is expressed through government or hierarchy, with state weakness also represented ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry); [ABC Entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 447).
- Psychology: The entries describe institutional power rather than a separate personal motive.
- Body/health: Neither entry establishes a specific medical condition.
- Social/relationship: Governments, ruling powers, and hidden hierarchy are explicit agents or settings.
- Events/manifestations: Oppression and criminal violence coexist in the range with deficient state influence and renunciation of force or violence.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC's `Autoritäts-Einfluß` means influence of authority; it does not independently specify authoritarianism. Its final `Verzicht des Staates auf Gewalt` means the state's renunciation of force or violence. The eight-clause row is verified on printed p. 447 (PDF p. 222); Witte's entry is on PDF p. 285.

## Contradictions

Oppressive government, weak government, and renunciation of force are different conditions. A uniformly brutal institution is not the only supported reading.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Vulcanus = Kronos`, Kronos specifies the Hades/Vulcanus pair through government and ruling hierarchy. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) emphasizes major crimes and heavy suppression by ruling powers. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 447) broadens the range to dangerous influence of authority, secret power, deficient state influence, lack of state power, and the state's renunciation of force or violence. Institutional coercion is central to Witte's examples, while the full page also allows weakness or restraint in the exercise of power.
