## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: PDF p. 285.

General dependence and suppression. Vices, obstacles, and dirt. General ugly manners and maneuvers. Raw and uncultured people. Terrible acts of violence in public life.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 446; PDF p. 222.

influence of the past in public life, energy deficiency in public, dangerous influence in public life, sorrow through violence in public life, renunciation of public force, and hidden power in public life

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Dependence, harmful influence, or violence appears in public life ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry); [ABC Entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 446).
- Psychology: ABC names sorrow through public violence; fear is not independently specified.
- Body/health: Neither entry establishes a specific bodily diagnosis.
- Social/relationship: Public conditions and general suppression are the explicit scale; Witte's classifying language remains attributed.
- Events/manifestations: Public violent acts, hidden power, energy deficiency, and renunciation of public force are listed.
- Conflicts/notes: Public scope does not mean every member of a society shares every manifestation. Both exact source rows are identified above.

## Contradictions

Violence in public life and renunciation of public force are alternative conditions. The entries support a range, not a uniformly aggressive society.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Hades/Vulcanus = Vernal Point`, Vernal Point specifies the Hades/Vulcanus pair through general conditions and public life. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) emphasizes dependence, suppression, and public acts of violence. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 446) adds hidden power, past influence, energy deficiency, and renunciation of public force. Harmful power may become a public concern, but the full range also includes weakness or restraint; these entries do not describe an entire society as uniformly violent.
