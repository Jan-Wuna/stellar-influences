## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 220; PDF p. 216.

To keep one’s equanimity under evil and viscous circumstances. Not to be intimidated by misfortune or threat.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 343; PDF p. 170.

Depression because of lack of success, success through thorough renunciation, consolidation of success from the past, consolidation of old rights, criminal circulation of money, disadvantages through blocked money, hardship through blocked rights, and deepening of happiness in secret.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Equanimity in adversity alongside restricted success and resources.
- Psychology: Witte's lack of intimidation contrasts with ABC's depression through unsuccessful effort.
- Body/health: Depression remains ABC's historical wording, not a clinical conclusion.
- Social/relationship: Old rights, financial disadvantage, and criminal circulation of money.
- Events/manifestations: Success through renunciation or past achievement; hidden happiness; blocked resources.
- Conflicts/notes: Neither composure nor depression is required in every case.

## Contradictions

Witte's composure and ABC's depressed response to unsuccessful effort differ materially; the entries do not explain when each applies.

The Witte scan prints "viscous" (printed p. 220, PDF p. 216). This anomalous adjective is retained literally; the synthesis relies on the explicit misfortune and threat wording instead of supplying a replacement.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Jupiter/Admetos = Hades`, Hades specifies the Jupiter/Admetos axis through composure or hardship amid adversity, financial blockage, and older rights. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) describes retaining equanimity under misfortune or threat. The [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) also admits hardship and depression when success, money or rights are blocked, while retaining success through renunciation and hidden happiness. The formula therefore concerns how constrained circumstances are borne and managed; composure is one supported response rather than proof that adversity has no cost.
