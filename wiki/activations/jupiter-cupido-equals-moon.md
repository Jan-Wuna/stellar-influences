## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: printed p. 215 (one-based PDF p. 211), Moon row.

Happy marriage or partnership of a woman, of people or of a nation. Cheerful hours in a large community. Happy hours in a ball-room. Cheerful group in the public.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 332 (one-based PDF p. 165), Moon row.

Success of a community of a people, a successful woman within the community, women's rights within the community, artistic success of a woman, a people's legal community, the community's sense of justice, and a contented community of a people.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Happy union and communal celebration, with successful participation, art, rights, and collective contentment.
- psychology: Cheerfulness, contentment, and the community's sense of justice.
- body/health: No bodily or medical condition is specified.
- social/relationship: A woman's happy union, a people or nation's partnership, women's rights, and successful participation within a community.
- events/manifestations: Ballroom enjoyment, cheerful public gatherings, artistic success of a woman, and a people's legal community.
- conflicts/notes: The seven-clause ABC Moon row is restored from the actual scan. Gendered examples remain historical source wording, not an exclusive application.

## Contradictions

The entries overlap in happy or contented community. ABC adds artistic success, women's rights, and a shared sense of justice; those extend Witte's celebrations without establishing that every happy gathering has the same legal structure.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Jupiter/Cupido = Moon`, Moon specifies the pair through a happy union, communal contentment, and cheerful public participation. Marriage or partnership, a ballroom, and a large public gathering give the shared happiness concrete settings. ABC extends participation into a woman's success in community or art, women's rights, and the community's sense of justice; these historical gendered examples sit alongside the broader collective range.
