## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 216; PDF p. 212.

To make indecent remarks. To tell dirty stories and obscene jokes. Vulgar talkativeness. Drivel and twaddle.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 334; PDF p. 166.

Reports about happiness in danger, successfully concealed thoughts, describing financial hardship, renouncing money in youth, juvenile crime and law, news about happiness in hardship, disadvantages through traffic law, and advantages and disadvantages of transport.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Coarse speech or communication about difficult resources and circumstances.
- psychology: Successfully concealed thoughts are explicit in ABC.
- body/health: No bodily condition is specified.
- social/relationship: Indecent remarks, obscene jokes, juvenile law and legal/transport topics.
- events/manifestations: Describing financial hardship, renouncing money in youth, and reports of happiness in danger.
- conflicts/notes: The manner of speech and its difficult subject matter are different; hardship talk is not inherently indecent.

## Contradictions

The sources differ between manner and subject of communication; neither identifies financial hardship itself with indecency.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Jupiter/Hades = Mercury`, Mercury specifies the Jupiter/Hades axis through speech, concealed thought and difficult subjects communicated. Witte judges coarse remarks, obscene jokes and trivial talk, while ABC also names financial hardship, youth and transport law, and reports of happiness in danger or need. These distinguish manner from subject: discussing hardship is not itself vulgarity. Renouncing money in youth remains a specific additional meaning. ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry); [ABC Entry](#abc-entry))
