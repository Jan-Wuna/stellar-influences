## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. `212`, printed p. `216`, Moon row; checked against the scan.

The disturbed happiness of a woman. People or female persons lack money. Happiness in the world of prostitutes. Women in economic poverty.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. `334`, one-based PDF p. `166`, Moon row; English translation checked against the German scan.

Traditional rights of the people, a woman's success through renunciation, a woman's success in old age, emotional distress over money, deficient rights of the woman, a deficient sense of justice, a woman's hidden happiness, and satisfaction through service to the people.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Disturbed or hidden happiness and material disadvantage, with distinct forms of satisfaction ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: ABC names emotional distress over money and a deficient sense of justice.
- body/health: No bodily or health claim is stated in these entries.
- social/relationship: The sources use historical gendered examples of women in poverty or finding success through renunciation.
- events/manifestations: Economic poverty, success in old age, hidden happiness and service to the people are separate source examples.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's former people-right, courageCummer and repeated woman-right wording was repaired from the Moon row; no domestic setting is specified.

## Contradictions

Witte's disturbed happiness and poverty coexist with ABC's possible success or satisfaction. These describe different conditions rather than a claim that deprivation always produces benefit. The gendered and prostitution examples remain historical source language.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Jupiter/Hades = Moon`, Moon specifies the pair through happiness and hardship experienced by people, especially women in these historical entries. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) directly connects disturbed happiness with lack of money; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds emotional distress, limited rights and a deficient sense of justice, but also hidden happiness, success through renunciation or in old age, and satisfaction through service. The formula therefore holds material disadvantage beside possible satisfaction without making either outcome inevitable.
