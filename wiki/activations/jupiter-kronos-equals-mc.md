## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: printed page 218; one-based PDF page 214, Jupiter/Kronos section, MC row.

To feel very happy. To consider oneself as the chief. Profession as an independent financial manager.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed page 338; one-based PDF page 168, Jupiter/Kronos section, MC row; full row and neighbors checked against the scan.

Special inner success, great inner joy, my success through independence, my money for the state, my great happiness, my important right, my special joy, my successful authority, my financial superiority, and my legal prestige

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal happiness and independent financial management in Witte; personal authority, rights and joy in ABC.
- psychology: Feeling very happy and considering oneself chief; inner success and joy.
- body/health: No bodily condition is stated.
- social/relationship: Independent financial manager in Witte; one's relation to state funds and legal prestige in ABC.
- events/manifestations: Independent success, financial superiority and successful personal authority.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's row is first-person; its former opening copied Vernal Point's public state-finance clause and its end was lost.

## Contradictions

The two entries share personal happiness and authority. Considering oneself chief is a stated self-view, not proof of actual supremacy or a guaranteed career position.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Jupiter/Kronos = MC`, [Witte](#witte-entry) joins feeling very happy and seeing oneself as chief with work as an independent financial manager. [ABC](#abc-entry) makes the perspective explicitly personal: inner joy, success through independence, one's authority, financial position and legal prestige. Money for the state and an important personal right add concrete contexts. This supports a personal experience of success and authority without assuming that the person occupies the top of an institution.
