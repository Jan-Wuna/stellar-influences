## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 212; one-based PDF p. 208, Jupiter + Uranus, oriented activator row.

Luck through things which were found or treasures of the earth such as minerals etc. Good fortune in or through evil activity.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 327; one-based PDF p. 162, Jupiter/Uranus, oriented activator row.

Exciting financial crime, success in the technical service, success impulses from the past, dangerous legal event, excitement about poor success, new advantages and disadvantages, sudden legal distress, and tensions by giving up money

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Fortune arises through found things, earth resources, or ethically troubling activity ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry)).
- Psychology: Excitement about inadequate success appears in ABC; a fixed disposition is not established.
- Body/health: No specific bodily or medical meaning is supplied.
- Social/relationship: Financial crime and legal distress are attributed source examples, not character accusations.
- Events/manifestations: Minerals, technical service, past opportunities, and giving up money are explicit ([ABC Entry](#abc-entry)).
- Conflicts/notes: The source's evil activity remains an evaluative historical phrase. ABC's financial crime, legal distress and giving up money are distinct examples, not a causal sequence.

## Contradictions

Material benefit and ethical or legal difficulty have different scopes; gain does not imply moral approval.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Jupiter/Uranus = Hades`, Hades specifies the pair's fortune through found objects, minerals, and difficult ethical contexts. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) explicitly includes both earth treasures and benefit through activity it calls evil; the [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) adds technical service, financial crime, and legal distress. Useful discovery and troubling gain remain distinct examples rather than a claim that all hidden or discarded things bring luck.
