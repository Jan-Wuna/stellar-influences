## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. 300, Kronos + Admetos, Moon row.

Peculiar misunderstood women. People who have a special position in public and who therefore behave differently.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 462; one-based PDF p. 230, Kronos + Admetos, Moon row.

Special perseverance of a woman, blocked independence of a people, end of a woman's independence, established authority of a woman, great depression of a people, great emotional deepening, thorough superiority of a woman, and superior emotional resistance.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** An exceptional public position combined with endurance or restricted independence.
- **Psychology:** Being misunderstood, perseverance, emotional deepening, and emotional resistance.
- **Body/health:** No physical condition is established; the depression wording remains historical source language.
- **Social/relationship:** Witte speaks of women and public figures; ABC also describes a people and female authority.
- **Events/manifestations:** Special public standing, constrained collective independence, and sustained authority.
- **Conflicts/notes:** The gender categories and collective depression language belong to the sources.

Evidence: [Witte entry](#witte-entry), PDF p. 300 (entry start); [ABC entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 462.

## Contradictions

Witte describes being socially set apart, while ABC adds endurance, constrained independence, and depth of feeling. These are different emphases; neither entry establishes that public distinction causes emotional restriction.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Kronos/Admetos = Moon`, Moon specifies the pair through people who are set apart by public position or whose independence is constrained. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) stresses being misunderstood; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds perseverance, established authority, and deepened or resistant feeling alongside collective discouragement. The sources describe women and a people in their historical vocabulary; their depression language does not establish a clinical diagnosis.
