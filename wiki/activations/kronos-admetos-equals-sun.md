## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. 300, Kronos + Admetos, Sun row.

The man who has to lead the life as an odd being, is all alone, who is different through his position, who is an exception, and who receives special treatment.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 462; one-based PDF p. 230, Kronos + Admetos, Sun row.

Special perseverance of a person, blocked independence of a man, end of personal independence, established personal authority, great depression of a person, thorough superiority of a man, and personal restrictions through the state.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** A person set apart by position or treatment, with endurance and restricted independence.
- **Psychology:** Perseverance and loneliness are represented; depression remains ABC's historical wording.
- **Body/health:** The entries do not establish a physical condition or clinical diagnosis.
- **Social/relationship:** Special treatment, personal authority, and restrictions imposed by the state.
- **Events/manifestations:** Living as an exception, losing independence, or maintaining established personal authority.
- **Conflicts/notes:** The sources' references to a man are historical categories, not a universal gender rule.

Evidence: [Witte entry](#witte-entry), PDF p. 300 (entry start); [ABC entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 462.

## Contradictions

Special standing may include authority or isolation; the entries do not require either to cause the other. Their male wording remains attributed.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Kronos/Admetos = Sun`, Sun specifies the pair through a person's exceptional position and the way that position governs independence. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes being alone, different, and specially treated; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds perseverance, established personal authority, and state restriction. The sources use male categories, but the bounded throughline is a person set apart by position, not an assertion about all men.
