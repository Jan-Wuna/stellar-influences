## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. 302 (one-based), Kronos + Poseidon, Moon entry.

#### Witte Entry

Persons or women who are mentally active and take part in cultural efforts. Representatives of the public education system in spiritual or secular terms. Cultural efforts in the public. Theater, concerts, lectures, courses.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. 466; PDF p. 232 (one-based), Kronos + Poseidon, Moon entry.

#### ABC Entry

A special feeling for truth; a woman's dominant insights; a people's intellectual hierarchy; a woman's intellectual superiority; a woman's great clarity; a clear feeling for what matters; a people's cultural standing; important truth for a woman.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Cultural participation and public education combine with a feeling for truth.
- Psychology: ABC emphasizes insight, clarity, and a feeling for what matters in historically gendered terms.
- Body/health: Neither entry makes a specific medical claim.
- Social/relationship: Women, mentally active people, a people's cultural standing, and education representatives appear explicitly.
- Events/manifestations: Theater, concerts, lectures, courses, and spiritual or secular education are named settings.
- Conflicts/notes: Gendered superiority and collective hierarchy remain attributed source language, not universal claims.

## Contradictions And Tensions

Gendered superiority and collective hierarchy remain attributed source language, not universal claims.

## Derived Synthesis

With Moon, Kronos/Poseidon describes active cultural participation and a receptive feeling for truth and significance. Witte makes the public range concrete through education representatives, theater, concerts, lectures, and courses. ABC connects personal insight and clarity with a people's cultural standing and intellectual hierarchy. Its repeated references to women, including intellectual superiority, remain historical gendered evaluations; they do not establish a general difference in ability. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
