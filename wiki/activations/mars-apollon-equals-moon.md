## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Persons who are active in trade, commerce or science. Business people. Trade and traffic in public life.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Success through work with feeling, impulses toward success from a woman, a people's will for freedom, commercial activity of a people, increased emotional actions, successes of a woman's will, a woman working scientifically, and increasing emotional impulses.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte Entry](#witte-entry), PDF p. 201 (exact activator row); [ABC Entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 316, PDF p. 157 (exact activator row).

- Core meaning: Trade and science are enacted by people and enter public life.
- Psychology: ABC includes work with feeling, emotional actions and impulses, and a people's will for freedom.
- Body/health: No distinct bodily claim is established by these occupational entries.
- Social/relationship: Witte names business people; ABC's woman-specific examples remain historical wording, not a universal gender rule.
- Events/manifestations: Public trade, traffic, and a woman working in science are explicit examples.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC combines collective activity with emotional and woman-specific examples; the latter remain attributed historical wording.

## Contradictions

The entries differ in emphasis; no direct contradiction is established for this exact orientation.

## Derived Synthesis

Moon specifies the Mars/Apollon axis through the people engaged in scientific and commercial activity and its place in public life. [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) emphasizes business people, trade, and traffic, while [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) adds a people's commercial activity and wish for freedom, work with feeling, emotional impulses, and a woman working in science. The latter is a source-specific example rather than a rule about who can perform this work; the emotional phrases describe the character of activity rather than a separate occupational restriction.
