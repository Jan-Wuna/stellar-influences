## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Betrothal with difficulties. Bad or mediocre results or work accomplishments. Fruits which one does not harvest. Abortion. Miscarriage.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Successful secret activity, success through renunciation of will, success impulses from the past, successful service work, good fortune in dangerous work, criminal legal actions, deficient labor rights, and deficient legal work.

- Source reference: printed p. 299, PDF p. 148, Hades row; the scan reads `Willens-Verzicht`, `Erfolgs-Impulse` and `Glück`, rather than the damaged cached fragments.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Results of activity may be impaired or arise through hidden, dangerous or service work.
- psychology: ABC names renunciation of will as one route to success.
- body/health: Witte's abortion and miscarriage examples remain historical claims, not medical predictions.
- social/relationship: Difficult betrothal and defective labor rights or legal work are explicit.
- events/manifestations: Poor or unharvested results coexist with secret success and successful service.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's favorable hidden/service outcomes differ materially from Witte's impaired results and must remain visible.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes disappointing results; ABC also names several successes, alongside legal defects. The entries support a mixed outcome range rather than failure alone.

## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies the Mars/Jupiter axis through difficulty or concealment in the results of action. [Witte](#witte-entry) emphasizes poor accomplishments, unharvested fruits and difficult betrothal. [ABC](#abc-entry) also retains success in secret or service work and good fortune in dangerous work, alongside defective rights and legal activity. The evidence therefore includes impaired results and less visible success; Witte's reproductive examples remain historical source claims rather than forecasts.

Evidence: Witte PDF p. 192; ABC printed p. 299, PDF p. 148.
