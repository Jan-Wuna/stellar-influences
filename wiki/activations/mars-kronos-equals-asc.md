## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

To bring about autocratic deeds with others. Interaction with people who are independent or act autocratically. To be threatened by the police or government. To be arrested.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. 314, PDF p. 156; English translation checked against the original German scan.

#### ABC Entry

Others act with self-confidence; a special workplace; dominant drive in the surroundings; independent actions of the partner; state work in the locality; priority work concerning the surroundings; important local work; the will of the authorities in the locality.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Independent or autocratic action is encountered through other people and the immediate locality.
- psychology: The entries emphasize self-directed or dominant conduct; they do not establish a clinical personality description.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is stated.
- social/relationship: Witte describes interaction with independent or autocratic people; ABC adds a partner's independent action and authority in the locality.
- events/manifestations: Witte includes police or government threats and arrest; ABC includes local state work and an important workplace.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's coercive examples are possibilities distinct from ordinary independent action. ABC's opening is scan-verified as self-confident action rather than self-employment.

## Contradictions

The entries range from independent conduct to coercive intervention. Their different intensity is a qualification, not a requirement that every encounter involves arrest.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Asc specifies the Mars/Kronos axis through direct dealings with other people and the nearby environment. [Witte](#witte-entry) includes both acting autocratically with others and encountering self-directed people; the scan-checked [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds the partner, workplace and authority operating locally.

Threat by government or police and arrest are Witte's coercive examples. They retain their specific source scope rather than defining every relationship described by this formula.

Evidence: [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 314, PDF p. 156; [Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 200 (entry start).
