## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

The man who acts autocratically or independently. Executive of the ruling powers as police officials.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

The chief's work centre, special bodily work, a person's dominant will, a personal will toward independence, a person acting independently, self-confident personal action, a state work centre, and a man's superior will.

- Source reference: printed p. 314, PDF p. 156, Sun row; `Selbständigkeit` concerns independence and `selbstbewußtes` means self-confident.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Independent or authoritative action is embodied in a person's work and will.
- psychology: Dominant will, desire for independence and self-confidence are ABC themes.
- body/health: Special bodily work is stated without a medical condition.
- social/relationship: A chief and police officials are source examples of acting within authority.
- events/manifestations: Independent personal action and state work centres are explicit settings.
- conflicts/notes: Male-specific source examples remain historical wording; neither independence nor confidence proves autocracy.

## Contradictions

The entries range from independent action to executing ruling power. These are distinct social roles rather than one required identity.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Sun specifies the Mars/Kronos axis through the person carrying out independent or authoritative action. [Witte](#witte-entry) names independent or autocratic conduct and police officials, while [ABC](#abc-entry) adds personal will toward independence, self-confidence and work centres. The entry distinguishes self-directed action from a formal executive role; its male-specific examples do not establish a universal gender rule.

Evidence: Witte PDF p. 200; ABC printed p. 314, PDF p. 156.
