## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Mars + Vulcanus,” PDF p. 203.

A man with great physical power. The physically active man. The man who is capable of violence.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Mars + Vulkanus,” printed p. 320 (PDF p. 159).

work of a center of power, influential personal work, will to power of a person, violent actions of a man, build bodily strength, forceful work of the body, personal will to power, personal strength of will, and center for work forces

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal physical power, active strength, and influential work.
- psychology: Personal willpower and will for power in ABC's entry.
- body/health: Physical activity and building bodily strength are named; no diagnosis is supplied.
- social/relationship: Historical male examples include capacity for violence, without establishing every strong person's conduct.
- events/manifestations: Forceful bodily work, a centre of power, and a centre for working forces.
- conflicts/notes: Gendered source language remains attributed; physical strength and violent action are distinct possibilities.

## Contradictions

Both entries include physical or personal strength and violence. ABC also supplies work, influence, and centres of power or labour.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mars/Vulcanus = Sun`, Sun specifies powerful effort through the body, personal will, and influential activity. Witte uses the historical example of a physically strong, active man; ABC adds building bodily strength, personal work, and a centre of power or working forces. Both include violence in their range, but neither physical capacity nor gender establishes that a person acts violently. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 203; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 320 / PDF p. 159)
