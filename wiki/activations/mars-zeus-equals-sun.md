## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Mars + Zeus,” PDF p. 199.

Energetic work on the day. The man who works goal-oriented. The machinist. Injuries by torch flame or by firearms.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Mars + Zeus,” printed p. 312 (PDF p. 155).

Strenuous bodily work, personally shaping work, a leading work centre, a man's will to lead, creative personal activity, bodily work achievement, personal work achievement, and personal leadership work.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Goal-directed personal activity, bodily work, and leadership.
- psychology: Will to lead and personally shape creative work.
- body/health: Witte historically includes torch-flame or firearm injuries; no individual outcome is established.
- social/relationship: Historical male and machinist examples remain source-specific.
- events/manifestations: Energetic work during the day, bodily achievement, and a leading work centre.
- conflicts/notes: Technical or physical work does not itself establish injury or violent conduct.

## Contradictions

The entries overlap on active personal work. ABC develops creative shaping and leadership, while Witte adds a machinist and specific injury examples.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mars/Zeus = Sun`, Sun specifies directed and creative effort through personal work, bodily achievement, and the will to lead. ABC emphasizes personally shaping the work and creative activity; Witte gives energetic daytime work and a goal-oriented man or machinist as historical examples. Witte's torch-flame and firearm injuries remain particular source associations rather than necessary consequences of bodily or technical effort. ([ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 312 / PDF p. 155; [Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 199)
