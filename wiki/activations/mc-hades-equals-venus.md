## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Meridian + Hades,” PDF p. 71.

Grief because of love. Vulgar lust and feelings. To be pleased with vulgar love.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Meridian + Hades,” printed p. 86 (PDF p. 42).

The secret of my beauty, I serve love, I love secrets, the soul's grief in love, my service secret, my beauty demands privations, beauty of an old soul, my past love, and a preference for the past.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal sorrow, service, secrecy, and the past expressed through love and beauty.
- psychology: Grief in love, attraction to secrets, and preference for the past.
- body/health: No medical effect is supplied.
- social/relationship: Serving love and past affection; Witte's judgments about vulgarity remain attributed.
- events/manifestations: Beauty requiring privations and love-related grief.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's service and old-soul beauty are not inherently degraded; Witte's moral vocabulary does not establish shame in every relationship.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes grief and vulgarity in love. ABC also includes service, beauty, secrecy, and the past, retaining a wider range than degraded sensuality.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `MC/Hades = Venus`, Venus specifies personal sorrow, service, and hidden matters through love and beauty. Witte names grief in love and uses historical judgments about vulgar feeling; ABC adds serving love, loving secrets, beauty that requires privation, and affection or beauty connected with the past. The shared grief theme therefore coexists with service and appreciation, without making every attachment shameful or degraded. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 71; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 86 / PDF p. 42)
