## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Meridian + Kronos,” PDF p. 73.

To be own cause of sorrows and obstacles. To obtain something from the earth. Owner of a mine. Owner of a blast furnace or melting plant.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Meridian + Kronos,” printed p. 91 (PDF p. 44).

Particular inner or soul grief, a great soul from the past, criminal domination over a soul, my lack of authority, my endangered state, my lack of independence, my renunciation of authority, my disadvantageous dominance, and inner or soul greatness in need.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal authority or independence meeting sorrow, deficiency, disadvantage, or material extraction.
- psychology: Inner grief, renunciation of authority, and greatness under hardship.
- body/health: No medical effect is supplied.
- social/relationship: Lack of authority or independence, harmful domination, and an endangered state.
- events/manifestations: Self-caused obstacles in Witte, mining or furnace ownership, and adverse dominance.
- conflicts/notes: Industrial ownership is distinct from criminal domination; the entries do not make all sorrow self-caused or all material work degrading.

## Contradictions

Witte includes self-caused sorrow and extracting materials from the earth. ABC adds lost authority, threatened independence, renunciation, and inner stature in need.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `MC/Kronos = Hades`, Hades specifies personal authority and independence through sorrow, disadvantage, or particular material enterprises. Witte names self-caused obstacles and obtaining things from the earth, with mines and furnaces as examples. ABC adds lack or renunciation of authority, an endangered state, harmful domination, and inner greatness in need. These retain material ownership and personal hardship as distinct expressions, without making industrial work inherently degrading or every sorrow self-caused. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 73; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 91 / PDF p. 44)
