## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Meridian + Kronos,” PDF p. 73.

An independent activity, or an action of one’s own judgement. (Foreman. Police captain. Officer. Sportsman. Boxer. Wrestler.)

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Meridian + Kronos,” printed p. 90 (PDF p. 44).

Efforts of a great soul, special inner or soul impulses, my self-confident actions, my work as chief, my work at an authority, my independent work, my important activity, important inner or soul work, and the will of my state.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal authority and independence expressed in action and work based on one's own judgment.
- psychology: Self-confident action and particular inner impulses.
- body/health: Sportspeople, boxers, and wrestlers appear as roles, without a medical effect.
- social/relationship: Chief, foreman, officer, police captain, and work at an authority among the examples.
- events/manifestations: Independent work, important activity, and the will of the state in ABC.
- conflicts/notes: Official and sporting roles exemplify independent action; they are not required occupations or evidence of violent character.

## Contradictions

Both entries emphasize independent action. ABC adds inner effort and chief/state work to Witte's list of practical and physical roles.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `MC/Kronos = Mars`, Mars specifies personal authority and independence through work and action based on one's own judgment. Witte illustrates this with foremen, officers, police captains, and sportspeople; ABC adds self-confident action, independent work, work as chief, and inner effort. The common thread is acting from personal judgment or responsibility, with official and physical roles remaining examples rather than prescribed occupations. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 73; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 90 / PDF p. 44)
