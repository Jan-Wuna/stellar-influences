## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Mercury + Apollon,” Sun entry, PDF p. 170.

Much exercise. Scientific success. The day of glory for a scientist.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Merkur + Apollon,” Sun entry, printed p. 262 / PDF p. 130. Translation checked against the German passage.

Success reports of a man, success of young people, successful body language, successful conversation centre, freedom of young people, personal experiences in youth, a centre for trading news, increasing passenger traffic.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal or scientific success and active expression.
- psychology: Personal youthful experience and freedom of young people.
- body/health: Much exercise and body language are source motifs, without a measured health outcome.
- social/relationship: Conversation, young people's freedom, and the source's male example.
- events/manifestations: Scientific recognition, success reports, a news-trading centre, and passenger traffic.
- conflicts/notes: The examples do not establish actual fitness, credentials, or recognition for every person; gendered language remains historical.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes exercise and scientific success, while ABC adds personal expression, youth, conversation and transport. These are distinct settings for activity, not one compulsory career or life outcome.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Apollon = Sun`, Sun specifies scientific thought and extensive communication through personal activity and expression. Witte joins much exercise with scientific success and a scientist's recognition; ABC adds body language, conversation, youthful experience and freedom, a news-trading centre and passenger traffic. The range connects active personal participation with successful exchange, without making scientific distinction or measured fitness an inevitable result. ABC's male example remains historical wording. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 170; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 262 / PDF p. 130)
