## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Mercury + Apollon,” Vulcanus entry, PDF p. 170.

Scientific news or information about energy or forces. Strong success through discourse with many people. Find understanding with many.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Merkur + Apollon,” Vulcanus entry, printed p. 263 / PDF p. 130. Translation checked against the German passage.

Spread of youth violence, influence expansion of youth, influence of scientific opinions, success through the power of the word, successful thought energy, opinion of science to violence, and multiplication of media power

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Influential scientific discourse and understanding reached with many.
- psychology: Mental energy and the power of expression.
- body/health: No bodily or medical effect is supplied.
- social/relationship: Expanding youthful influence and the influence of scientific opinions.
- events/manifestations: Scientific energy/force information, success through speech, and increased media power.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's youth-violence and science-on-violence examples remain attributed; they do not identify a person as violent or make discourse coercive.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes success and understanding through discourse. ABC also names influence and media power, with violence as a particular subject or example; the entry does not equate understanding with domination.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Apollon = Vulcanus`, Vulcanus specifies scientific thought and extensive communication through the influence of speech and ideas. Witte joins information about energy or forces with success and understanding reached through discourse with many; ABC adds the power of words, scientific opinion and increased media power. The connection is effective or influential exchange, without making comprehension a form of coercion. ABC's separate violence-related examples remain historical source claims rather than a judgment about the speaker. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 170; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 263 / PDF p. 130)
