## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Ascendant + Mercury,” Admetos entry, PDF p. 83.

Serious and deep talks. To say very little, to save words, to be taciturn.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Aszendent + Merkur,” Admetos entry, printed p. 107 / PDF p. 52. Translation checked against the German passage.

Continuing conversations of the partner, blocked conversation partners, blocked youth at the place, blocked thinking of others, depressed youth in the surroundings, the partner's depth of thought, thorough description of the partner, circulation of thoughts at the place.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Serious or deep exchange, economical speech, and restricted communication.
- psychology: Depth of thought and blocked thinking in relation to others.
- body/health: ABC's depressed-youth wording is historical attribution, not a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: Sustained partner conversation and blocked conversation partners.
- events/manifestations: Thorough description of a partner and local circulation of thought.
- conflicts/notes: Depth or sustained conversation can coexist with sparse speech; no hidden motive for silence is supplied.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes deep talks and few words; ABC also includes sustained partner conversations beside blockage. These are different forms of limited or concentrated exchange, not a fixed silent personality.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Asc = Admetos`, Admetos specifies communication with others and the immediate surroundings through depth, sustained attention and limits on expression. Witte joins serious talks with saving words; ABC adds continued partner conversation, thorough description and depth of thought beside blocked partners or thinking. The relation includes concentrated exchange and difficulty communicating, without inventing guarded motives or making every instance taciturn. ABC's depressive wording remains attributed. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 83; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 107 / PDF p. 52)
