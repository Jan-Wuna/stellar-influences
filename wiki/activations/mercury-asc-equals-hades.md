## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source: “Ascendant + Mercury,” Hades entry, PDF p. 83.

Others are mistaken. Evil gossip. Slanderous talk, calumniation concerning oneself. Common talks about the far past.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source: “Aszendent + Merkur,” Hades entry, printed p. 107 / PDF p. 52. Translation checked against the German passage.

Old-fashioned thoughts of the partner, describing others' distress, dangerous thinking at the place, conversations about polluted surroundings, criminal youth in the surroundings, sorrowful thoughts of the partner, lack of conversation partners, deficient partner conversations.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Difficult or mistaken communication, sorrowful thought, and discussion of the past.
- psychology: A partner's sorrow or old-fashioned thoughts; error is attributed to others in Witte.
- body/health: No distinct medical outcome is supplied; distress and polluted surroundings are subjects of discussion.
- social/relationship: Gossip, slander, missing conversation partners, and deficient partner exchange.
- events/manifestations: Description of others' distress and conversations about the distant past or polluted surroundings.
- conflicts/notes: The partner's sorrow is not treachery. Criminal and slander examples remain source claims, not grounds for accusing a person.

## Contradictions

Witte includes mistaken or slanderous talk, but also common conversation about the far past. ABC adds sorrow, distress and insufficient exchange; these do not make every historical or difficult conversation malicious.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Asc = Hades`, Hades specifies communication with others and the immediate surroundings through difficult subjects, error or inadequate exchange. Witte names gossip and slander alongside conversation about the distant past; ABC adds a partner's sorrowful thoughts, describing others' distress and a shortage of conversation partners. Sorrow and lack of exchange differ from malicious speech, so the historical or criminal examples do not establish that a partner is treacherous or a conversation harmful. ([Witte](#witte-entry), PDF p. 83; [ABC](#abc-entry), printed p. 107 / PDF p. 52)
