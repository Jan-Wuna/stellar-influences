## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `ASCENDANT + MERCURY`
- Source locator: PDF p. `83` (oriented entry; scan checked).

#### Witte Entry

To be very much occupied with professional or business talks. To be hurried.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Aszendent + Merkur`
- Source locator: printed p. `107`, PDF p. `52` (entry).
- Text basis: translation checked against the raw scan on PDF p. `52`; supplied parser-normalized OCR was not itself visually verified.

#### ABC Entry

Others speak about power, reports about places of power, influential conversation partners, energetic local conversations, describing violence in the surroundings, violent local youth, the partner's powerful language, and local news about violence.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Communication with others becomes forceful, influential, or heavily occupied with business.
- psychology: Hurry is named by Witte; the entries do not establish a general personality trait.
- body/health: No bodily or health statement is supplied.
- social/relationship: Influential partners and powerful speech appear alongside reports of violence in the environment.
- events/manifestations: Professional or business talks, reports about places of power, and news about violence are named.
- conflicts/notes: Reports about violence do not establish that the speaker is violent. Witte's hurry and ABC's power vocabulary retain their separate scope.

## Contradictions

Heavy business communication and conversation about force are different emphases, not a direct contradiction.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Asc = Vulcanus`, Vulcanus specifies communication with others through intensity or power. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes being heavily occupied with professional or business talks and feeling hurried; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 107) adds influential conversation partners, forceful speech, and reports about power or violence. These support pressure and powerful subject matter without turning a report of violence into a claim about the speaker's conduct.
