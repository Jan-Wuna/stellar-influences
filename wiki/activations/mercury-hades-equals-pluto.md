## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- PDF page (entry start): `167`.

#### Witte Entry

A thinking faculty that cannot be developed. People who barely progress mentally.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Printed source page: `257`; PDF page: `127`.

#### ABC Entry

German passage (transcribed from PDF page `127`, printed page `257`):

> Gefährliche Entwicklung in der Jugend, kummervolle Gedanken entwickeln, nachteilige Jugend-Entwicklung, Veränderungen durch Bewegungs-Mangel, wachsende Gespräche über die Vergangenheit, wachsende Kriminalität der Jugend

Editorial translation from the German passage: Dangerous development in youth; developing sorrowful thoughts; adverse development of young people; changes through lack of movement; increasing conversations about the past; increasing youth criminality.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Limited mental progress and troubling developments in youth ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: Witte names little mental progress; ABC adds developing sorrowful thoughts.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is established; the ABC list mentions lack of movement.
- social/relationship: ABC separately names adverse youth development and increasing youth criminality.
- events/manifestations: ABC names increasing conversation about the past and changes through lack of movement.
- conflicts/notes: The complete Pluto row has been restored from its own glyph; its beginning was previously absorbed into Neptune.

## Contradictions

The scan-verified ABC passage has been restored with its distinct clauses. These examples supplement Witte's entry without establishing that all listed conditions occur together.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Hades = Pluto`, Pluto specifies Mercury/Hades through difficulty in development. Witte emphasizes thinking that makes little progress, while ABC adds adverse or dangerous development in youth and the growth of sorrowful thoughts. Increasing discussion of the past and changes through lack of movement are further listed manifestations. These remain the authors' distinct associations, rather than a clinical finding or a claim that development is permanently impossible ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
