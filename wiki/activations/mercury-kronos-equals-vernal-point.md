## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- PDF page (entry start): `169`.

#### Witte Entry

General uprightness. Upon good faith. Government news that affect the general public or the world.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Printed source page: `260`; PDF page: `129`.

#### ABC Entry

prestige of youth in public, dominance of youth in public life, conversations about public authority, great traffic in public, media dominance in public life, and public opinion of the state

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Important official communication in public life ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: Witte names uprightness and good faith as attributed ideals.
- body/health: No bodily condition appears.
- social/relationship: The general public, youth standing, and media dominance are named.
- events/manifestations: Government news affecting the public or world is Witte's concrete example.
- conflicts/notes: Public consequence does not establish an authority's honesty.

## Contradictions

Witte's good-faith wording is an ideal within the entry; ABC's public dominance concerns influence. Their presence together does not make every dominant official message upright.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Mercury/Kronos = Vernal Point`, Vernal Point specifies important thought through the public reach of authority and official news. Government messages affect the general public or world, while ABC adds public authority, youth standing, and media dominance. Witte's uprightness and good faith remain the source's accompanying ideals, distinct from the fact that a message has wide consequences ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
