## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source locator: PDF p. 160, one-based, entry start (supplied source-evidence record; not a printed-page number).

Babble. Abusive words. Cries for help. To be ready for a misdeed.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

German passage (transcribed from PDF page `120`, printed page `243`):

> Alte Schriften bearbeiten, erschwerte Arbeits-Besprechungen, gefährliche Medien-Tätigkeit, Gespräche über Nachteile durch Arbeit, Gespräche über Verzicht auf Arbeit, kriminelle Jugend-Tätigkeit, kummervolle Gedanken-Arbeit, mangelhafte Jugend-Arbeit

Editorial translation from the German passage: Working on old writings; difficult work discussions; dangerous media activity; conversations about disadvantages through work; conversations about forgoing work; criminal youth activity; sorrowful mental work; deficient youth work.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Speech and work under distress, difficulty, or harmful use.
- psychology: Witte includes readiness for a misdeed; this is a source possibility, not a character verdict.
- body/health: Cries for help signal distress in Witte, without specifying illness.
- social/relationship: Abusive words, difficult work meetings, and discussion of disadvantages or giving up work.
- events/manifestations: Work on old writings, dangerous media activity, and criminal youth activity in ABC.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's sorrowful mental work is distinct from criminal activity; work on old writings is not itself harmful.

## Contradictions

Witte includes both cries for help and readiness for wrongdoing. ABC adds sorrowful mental work and work on old writings, preserving distress, harm, and historical work as separate examples.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies Mercury/Mars through distressed or damaging communication: the [Witte entry](#witte-entry) runs from babble and abusive words to cries for help and readiness for wrongdoing. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) also includes difficult work discussions, but preserves the less dramatic example of editing old writings. These are distinct manifestations of troubled or difficult speech and work; the entry does not justify describing a person as inherently harmful.
