## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Evidence locator: one-based PDF p. 135 (entry start); source heading: `Moon + Admetos`.

#### Witte Entry

Narrow minded. To live in crowded conditions. Mentally impressed by narrowness of space of the own people. The minute of death.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Evidence locator: printed p. 202; one-based PDF p. 100 (entry); source heading: `Mond + Admetos`.

#### ABC Entry

German passage (transcribed from PDF page `100`, printed page `202`):

> Ausdauer meiner Frau, Blockade einer gefühlvollen Seele, Einschränkung meiner Gefühle, gefestigte Frauen-Seele, Kreislauf meiner Frau, meine Frau ist gründlich, meine Gefühle sind blockiert, meine Frau ist bedrückt, Widerstand meiner Frau

Editorial translation from the German passage: My wife's endurance; blockage of a feeling soul; restriction of my feelings; a steadfast woman's soul; my wife's circulation; my wife is thorough; my feelings are blocked; my wife is oppressed in mood; my wife's resistance.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Constricted conditions are experienced personally.
- psychology: Witte gives narrow thinking and being mentally impressed by crowded conditions.
- body/health: Witte's death phrase and ABC's circulation clause remain attributed historical associations.
- social/relationship: ABC's first-person partner examples are gendered and source-specific.
- events/manifestations: Living in crowded conditions is the clearest concrete Witte example.
- conflicts/notes: Circulation and blocked feeling are distinct from Witte's literal death phrase; no timing rule is derived.

## Contradictions

The death association is not a demonstrated timing rule; no timing layers are added.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Admetos = MC`, MC specifies a personal experience of confinement. [Witte](#witte-entry) emphasizes being mentally affected by one's people's lack of space; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds restricted personal feelings and endurance within gendered examples. Witte also lists 'the minute of death'; that historical assertion remains attributed and does not establish a method for predicting death.
