### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source passage: `Mond + Hades`; printed p. `195`; one-based PDF page `96` (Cupido row checked against the scan).

Woman from a shared past, shared service to women, shared emotional sorrow, community of older women, lack of feeling in the community, hour of danger for a community, a people's community of tradition.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Shared hardship, emotional sorrow and danger within family or communal life, alongside service and a common past.
- psychology: Shared sorrow and a lack of feeling within the community are explicit ABC themes.
- body/health: Widowhood and a 'fatal hour' are historical examples, not mortality predictions.
- social/relationship: Widowhood, communities of older women, service to women and shared traditions are distinct settings.
- events/manifestations: A family's misfortune and a community's hour of danger appear beside shared service and historical continuity.
- conflicts/notes: Witte emphasizes widowhood and family misfortune; ABC also preserves shared history, service and tradition. Its former policy/cohesion sentence was replaced from the actual Cupido row.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes poverty, widowhood and a family's misfortune, while ABC includes communities of older women, shared service and tradition beside emotional lack and danger. These are different examples, not a demonstrated contradiction or an inevitable outcome; repeated traditional wording does not establish independent corroboration.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Hades = Cupido`, Cupido specifies hardship and the past through shared family or community life. A common history, service to women and communities of older women can hold people together, while shared sorrow, emotional lack or danger describe difficult communal experiences. ABC's traditional community and Witte's poor widow and her leisure preserve quieter social settings alongside the severe family-misfortune image. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry))

The entry therefore supports both shared service and the burdens carried within a group. Widowhood and a family's fatal hour remain historical examples, not a prediction of bereavement or a claim that every traditional community is distressed.
