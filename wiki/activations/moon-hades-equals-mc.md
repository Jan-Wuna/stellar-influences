## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source passage: `Moon + Hades`; one-based PDF page `131` (MC row checked against the scan).

Defamation through malicious women. To be enmeshed by bad women. Fiancee of low standards. Slandered, worried. Moment of loneliness or difficult circumstances for females. Tragically moment for the public.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source passage: `Mond + Hades`; printed p. `194`; one-based PDF page `96` (MC row checked against the scan).

Danger for my wife, danger for me in the hour, my emotional sorrow, my people in need, my renunciation of the woman, soul of a woman who serves, emotional distress of a people, emotional service to the woman, my people's past.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Hardship, danger and sorrow are personally experienced, including concern for one's wife or people.
- psychology: Worry, loneliness, one's own emotional sorrow and a people's emotional distress are explicit.
- body/health: No medical condition is established by the displayed entries.
- social/relationship: Witte describes slander involving women; ABC adds service to a woman, a woman who serves and one's relationship to a people in need. Witte's moral labels are historical judgments.
- events/manifestations: Personal danger, difficult circumstances, renunciation, collective need and the past of one's people are distinct examples beside Witte's tragic public moment.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's former damaged soul list omitted the opening danger and sorrow clauses; the scan confirms one serving woman's soul and a people's emotional distress, not several additional souls.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes slander, worry and loneliness; ABC adds personally felt danger, service, renunciation and concern for one's people and their past. These differences do not establish a contradiction. The examples do not establish one inevitable outcome, and shared traditional phrasing is not independent corroboration.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Hades = MC`, MC specifies hardship as personally felt danger, sorrow or difficult circumstances. Concern can center on one's wife, a people in need or the past of one's people; service to a woman and the inner life of a woman who serves place care beside distress. Witte's worry and loneliness belong to this personal experience, while his tragic public moment and ABC's collective emotional distress extend it to a people. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry))

Slander, renunciation and danger are separate source examples, not a single inevitable story. Witte's moral judgments about women remain attributed historical wording rather than general character claims.
