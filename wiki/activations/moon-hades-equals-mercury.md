## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source passage: `Moon + Hades`; one-based PDF page `131` (exact oriented entry checked against actual scan).

A brooding poor woman. Diseased brains. Worried thoughts. Stupidity.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source passage: `Mond + Hades`; printed p. `194`; PDF page `96` (complete oriented row checked against actual scan).

Reports from a people's past, danger for young women, lack of feeling among youth, conversations about endangered women, emotional grief among youth, media criminality of a people, reports of a people in need, criminality among female youth.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Worry appears in thought, conversation and reports about hardship.
- psychology: Brooding and worried thoughts coexist with emotional grief and lack of feeling among youth in ABC.
- body/health: Witte's 'diseased brains' is a historical medical assertion, not a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: Talk about endangered women and reports of a people in need are ABC examples.
- events/manifestations: Historical reporting and media-related crime are listed manifestations.
- conflicts/notes: The scan distinguishes emotional deficiency and grief among youth from the separate media/female-youth criminality clauses; Witte's derogatory and medical wording remains attributed.

## Contradictions

No direct contradiction is established. The historical examples remain separate possibilities rather than diagnoses or character conclusions.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Hades = Mercury`, hardship is carried in thought and communication: brooding and worry accompany reports about a people's past or need and conversations about endangered women. Lack of feeling and emotional grief among youth are distinct from the separate media and female-youth criminality examples. Witte's diseased-brains and stupidity phrases remain historical medical and value judgments, without turning these descriptions into diagnoses or conclusions about a person's intelligence. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry).)
