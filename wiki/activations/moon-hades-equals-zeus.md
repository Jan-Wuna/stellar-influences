## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Hardship is associated with difficult effort, leadership or destructive conflict.
- psychology: ABC names striving and constrained achievement rather than a fuller psychological account.
- body/health: War widowhood and destruction are historical examples, not mortality predictions.
- social/relationship: Prisoners of war and war widows are Witte's traditional social settings.
- events/manifestations: Destruction by fire, demanding service, impaired leadership and hidden achievements.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's war imagery and ABC's demanding work are different scales of difficulty; criminal aims are one attributed alternative.

## Contradictions

Witte's war and fire examples are more destructive than ABC's demanding service, constrained creativity and hidden achievement. These are different scales of difficulty; the entries do not establish that every constrained effort has a violent or criminal purpose.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Hades = Zeus`, Zeus specifies hardship through difficult effort and, in Witte's severe examples, the destructive consequences of war or fire. ABC adds demanding service, disadvantages for creative women, weak leadership and hidden achievement. These can be read as constrained or costly activity without making every demanding task a sign of war or criminal purpose. ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry))
