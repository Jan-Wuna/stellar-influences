## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: one-based PDF p. 60, complete Sun row under Meridian + Moon checked.

#### Witte Entry

The mental influence on the body. The glandular system. Daily mental and emotional influences. The body condition is influenced by the state of mind.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 64; one-based PDF p. 31, complete nine-clause Sun row checked.

#### ABC Entry

Editorial translation checked against the German scan: A soulful female body; a man's soulful emotional nature; my husband's emotional nature; a man's feelingful soul; the emotional nature and soul of a person; myself as a man's wife; my feelings as a man; the psyche of man and woman; the soul of a people's body.

### Reinhold Ebertin - The Combination of Stellar Influences

- Source locator: printed p. 143; one-based PDF p. 145, coded entry `0447`, complete entry checked.

#### Ebertin Entry

A masculine soul, a positive attitude to life, vigor, enterprising spirit, tie or attachment of the ego both in body and soul, a strong soul link with the mother or wife.

### John Sandbach - Midpoints: A Kabbalistic Compendium of Meanings for Astrological Midpoints

- Source locator: one-based PDF p. 40, complete Sun paragraph under the Moon/Midheaven chapter beginning on PDF p. 40 checked.

#### Sandbach Entry

One’s purpose in life centers on clearing the emotional body so that exalted feelings may shine forth. The ability to remain constant and relatively undisturbed by the ever-changing conditions that occur in one’s career.

### Michael Munkasey - Midpoints: Unleashing the Power of the Planets

- Source heading: `MOON/MIDHEAVEN with Planets and Points`
- Source page: printed `144`; one-based PDF p. 145, complete `Sun` row checked.

#### Munkasey Entry

Defining your own character and growing beyond early family influences; the purpose and determination you set for yourself in defining who you are and what you will do with your life; the importance of family identification.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal identity and purpose joined with emotional life and bodily experience. [ABC](#abc-entry); [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry).
- psychology: A positive attitude, vigor, self-definition and constancy amid changing circumstances are stated. [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [Sandbach](#sandbach-entry).
- body/health: Witte names the glandular system and mental influence on bodily condition; these remain attributed historical claims, not a diagnosis. [Witte](#witte-entry).
- social/relationship: A strong mother/wife bond and family identification coexist with developing beyond early family influences. ABC gives gendered personal and collective examples. [Ebertin](#ebertin-entry); [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry).
- events/manifestations: Defining life aims and maintaining constancy through career changes are represented; public leadership is not specified. [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry); [Sandbach](#sandbach-entry).
- conflicts/notes: Family attachment and self-definition are distinct themes; gendered wording does not prescribe identity or a universal family role.

## Contradictions

Ebertin emphasizes attachment to mother or wife; Munkasey includes both family identification and growth beyond early family influence. Sandbach emphasizes constancy despite changing career conditions, rather than inevitable mood-driven instability.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/MC = Sun`, personal identity and purpose are closely connected with the inner emotional life. The restored [ABC entry](#abc-entry) gives several distinct body, feeling and relationship examples, while the [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes mental and emotional influence on bodily condition. The [Ebertin entry](#ebertin-entry) adds vigor, a positive attitude and strong family bonds. The [Munkasey](#munkasey-entry) and [Sandbach](#sandbach-entry) entries develop self-definition and constancy: family remains significant, but one's aims can become one's own and need not fluctuate with changing career circumstances. These themes do not establish public leadership or a compulsory gender role.
