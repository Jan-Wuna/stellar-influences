## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Moon + Mercury`
- Source reference: PDF p. `122` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Depressed mind. Sorrowful thinking. Forgetting. To drop a thought.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Mond + Merkur`
- Source reference: printed p. `177`, PDF p. `87` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Blocked thinking of a woman, reports of subjugation of a people, depressed young woman, restriction of women's opinion, strike movements in the people, deep feelings of youth, subjugated youth of a people, and subjugated popular opinion

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: thought and expression become blocked, sorrowful, forgotten, or suppressed ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: sorrowful thinking, dropping a thought, and deep feelings among youth are distinct expressions in the entries.
- body/health: no specific bodily condition is stated; depressed thinking is descriptive source language, not a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: the ABC entry extends inhibition to women's opinions, youth, and public opinion.
- events/manifestations: forgetting, reports of oppression, and strikes appear among the concrete examples.
- conflicts/notes: personal discouragement and collective suppression are different scales of the formula; the historical gendered examples do not establish a universal claim about women.

## Contradictions

The two entries do not state a direct contradiction. Their emphasis differs between sorrowful or abandoned thought and the suppression of expression in a population; this does not establish independent corroboration.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Mercury = Admetos`, Admetos specifies the Moon/Mercury pair through thought that stalls, is abandoned, or becomes burdened by sorrow. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) names forgetting and dropping a thought; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) carries restriction into blocked thinking and suppressed opinions, while also retaining deep feelings among youth (printed p. 177, PDF p. 87).

The common thread is difficulty sustaining or expressing thought, at either a personal or collective scale. Reports of oppression and strikes belong to the ABC's social examples; they should not be reduced to an individual's mood or treated as a medical prediction.
