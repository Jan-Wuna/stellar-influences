## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: thought, language, and youth association take shape within a community ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: artistic thinking is one specific Witte example; the entries do not supply a wider account of individual psychology.
- body/health: no specific bodily or health implication is stated.
- social/relationship: youth associations, women's communities, and marriage are explicit social forms.
- events/manifestations: shared language, group conversations, community news, and the marriage of young girls are source examples.
- conflicts/notes: the marriage wording is historical and attributed; the entries do not establish general courtship behavior or a universal rule for girls.

## Contradictions

These entries do not state a direct contradiction. Witte's marriage and artistic examples are narrower than the ABC's communal communication; they should remain visible rather than being treated as interchangeable claims.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Moon/Mercury = Cupido`, Cupido specifies the Moon/Mercury pair through shared language and association within a community. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) repeatedly places conversations, opinions, youth, and news in a communal setting (printed p. 177, PDF p. 87). The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) also names youth associations, while retaining artistic thinking and the historically specific example of young girls' marriage.

The unifying theme is communication and participation within a social body. Art and marriage remain particular source examples, rather than a basis for inferring courtship rituals or a general preference for a group's tastes.
