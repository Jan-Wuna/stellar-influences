## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Moon + Mercury`
- Source reference: PDF p. `122` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Bad memory. Garbage-wagon. Street walkers. Not settled people. Poor girls. Depressed mind.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Mond + Merkur`
- Source reference: printed p. `177`, PDF p. `87` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Endangered youth of a people, a young woman's secret, conversations about emotional distress, criminal youth of a people, news about privations of a people, hidden thoughts of a woman, and female youth crime

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: troubled thought and communication about distress, concealment, or deprivation ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- psychology: Witte names poor memory and a depressed mind; ABC adds concealed thoughts and emotional distress.
- body/health: the entries provide no specific bodily condition or basis for diagnosis.
- social/relationship: endangered youth, poverty, and marginal social circumstances are source examples, not judgments about a person's character.
- events/manifestations: a garbage wagon, reports of deprivation, secrets, and youth crime accompany the mental themes.
- conflicts/notes: German gefährdete means endangered, not dangerous; the ABC separately mentions criminal youth. Witte's historical social labels remain attributed.

## Contradictions

There is no direct contradiction stated between these entries. Memory difficulties, hidden thought, and collective hardship are different examples; their association does not make vulnerability evidence of criminality.

## Derived Synthesis

In Moon/Mercury = Hades, Hades specifies the Moon/Mercury pair through troubled or concealed thought and communication about hardship. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) names poor memory and a depressed mind, while the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds conversations about emotional distress, secrets, and news of deprivation (printed p. 177, PDF p. 87). Its endangered youth and criminal youth are separate examples, not interchangeable descriptions.

Witte's garbage wagon and historical social labels remain concrete source examples. They do not warrant describing a person's mind or social surroundings as dirty or morally degraded.
