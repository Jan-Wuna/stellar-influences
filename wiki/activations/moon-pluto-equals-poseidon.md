## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source passage: `Moon + Pluto`, PDF p. `129` (entry start; printed page not supplied).

#### Witte Entry

Change of mental attitude. Events caused by fast changes or mutations.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source passage: `Mond + Pluto`, printed p. `191`, PDF p. `94` (row and English translation checked against the German scan).

#### ABC Entry

Development of a cultured woman, development of truth by a woman, mental development of a woman, developing clarity of feelings, mental changes among the people, change in a people's culture, and growing knowledge of a woman.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: change is expressed through mental attitude, cultural development, and greater clarity of feeling.
- psychology: a changing outlook and growing understanding are represented.
- body/health: neither entry gives a specific bodily or health manifestation.
- social/relationship: ABC names women's intellectual development and changes in a people's culture.
- events/manifestations: Witte includes events caused by rapid changes; ABC emphasizes developing knowledge and culture.
- conflicts/notes: speed is explicit in Witte, but the ABC development motifs do not establish that all understanding arrives suddenly.

## Contradictions

- No direct incompatibility is stated. Witte emphasizes changing attitude and rapid events; ABC supplies intellectual and cultural forms of development without specifying the same pace.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Poseidon specifies the Moon/Pluto pair through changes of outlook and understanding. Witte names a change in mental attitude and events produced by rapid change; ABC gives this development a more particular intellectual and cultural range, including clarity of feelings, growing knowledge, and changes in a people's culture ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 191). The entries support a changing interpretation of experience, while leaving the pace and outcome of that development open.
