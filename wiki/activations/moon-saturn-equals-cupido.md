## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `126` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

The marriage of a widow. Hours of relaxation in the evening. Death-cult of a nation.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `185`, PDF p. `91` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Tasks of a women's community, burdens of a people's community, a serious hour for a community, a patient women's community, disturbed folk art, problems of a people's community, mature art of a woman, and a woman separated from the community

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: community or family life marked by serious tasks, patience, separation, or mature art.
- psychology: patience is named without a fixed personality description.
- body/health: Witte's death-cult example concerns collective practice rather than a personal health diagnosis.
- social/relationship: a widow's marriage, women's communities, and separation from a group are examples.
- events/manifestations: evening relaxation, mature art, disturbed folk art, and collective tasks are stated.
- conflicts/notes: evening leisure is not identical with loss; the historical national death-cult example remains a distinct claim.

## Contradictions

Witte combines a widow's marriage and evening leisure with a national death-cult; ABC combines community burdens with patience and mature art. Their different settings need not form one sequence or one uniformly solemn social experience.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Moon/Saturn = Cupido, Cupido specifies the pair through shared life in which responsibility, patience, or separation matters. [ABC](#abc-entry) names community tasks and burdens, patient women's groups, mature art, and a woman separated from a community (printed p. 185, PDF p. 91). [Witte](#witte-entry) adds a widow's marriage, evening relaxation, and a nation's death-cult. The personal, artistic, and ritual examples retain their own settings rather than describing one necessary form of collective grief.
