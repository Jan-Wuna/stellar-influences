## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Excitement about malice. Violent woman. Murderess. Violent mob.

Source locator: PDF p. 127.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Agitation through emotional sorrow, disadvantage suffered by a nervous woman, criminal technique of a people, new deprivations for the people, new criminality among the people, sudden difficulties for a woman, unrest through a reform affecting the people.

Translation checked against the German scan: printed p. 187, PDF p. 92, Hades row. “Gemüts-Kummer” is emotional sorrow and “Erschwernisse” means difficulties, not silence.

## Comparative Schema

- **Core meaning:** Agitation associated with sorrow, deprivation, malice, or violence ([Witte](#witte-entry); [ABC](#abc-entry)).
- **Psychology:** Emotional distress and nervousness in ABC; excitement over malice in Witte.
- **Body/health:** Violence appears as a source example, but the entries supply no medical diagnosis.
- **Social/relationship:** Disadvantage and sudden difficulties affecting a woman; collective unrest, criminality, and mob violence.
- **Events/manifestations:** New deprivation or criminality, unrest over reform, and Witte's extreme violence examples.
- **Conflicts/notes:** “Violent woman” and “Murderess” are historical source labels, not judgments warranted about a person from this formula.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes malice and extreme violence, while ABC includes sorrow, disadvantage, and hardship. These differ in severity and subject; the page does not establish that distress implies criminal behavior.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies Moon/Uranus through agitation bound up with hardship or harmful circumstances. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) connects unrest with emotional sorrow, deprivation, disadvantage, and sudden difficulties; the [Witte entry](#witte-entry) gives the more extreme examples of malice and violence.

The common thread is disturbed feeling or collective excitement in a damaging setting. The source's criminal and gendered labels must remain attributed examples: the formula does not justify treating emotional distress as evidence of violence or criminality.
