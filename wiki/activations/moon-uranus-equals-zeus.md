## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `127` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Hours of tense creating power. To be forced by an uproarious people. Strong willed populace or nation in war.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `187`, PDF p. `92` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

A strenuous hourly rhythm, dynamic creativity of women, an exciting hour of procreation, fiery emotional impulses, new leadership of a people, a woman's creative impulses, technical achievements of a people, and a procreation event for a woman

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: tense creative activity, strong collective will, and forceful impulses toward making or leading.
- psychology: fiery emotional impulses and creative impulses are stated.
- body/health: ABC names procreation as a historical association without establishing fertility or a health condition.
- social/relationship: pressure from an uproarious people, leadership, and a nation in war are examples.
- events/manifestations: strenuous rhythms, technical achievements, creation, and procreation events are listed.
- conflicts/notes: creative power and coercive collective pressure are distinct possibilities; neither ensures productive success.

## Contradictions

Witte's war and pressure from a populace carry a different evaluation from ABC's creativity and technical achievement. The entries share forceful activity without establishing that unrest must become productive.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Moon/Uranus = Zeus, Zeus specifies the pair through tense creating power and forceful emotional or collective impulses. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) retains pressure from an uproarious people and a strong-willed nation in war. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds dynamic creativity, fiery emotional impulses, leadership, technical achievement, and a strenuous rhythm of hours (printed p. 187, PDF p. 92).

ABC's procreation examples remain specific historical associations. The creative and coercive settings give this orientation a range of active expressions, without establishing that collective excitement will lead to useful production.
