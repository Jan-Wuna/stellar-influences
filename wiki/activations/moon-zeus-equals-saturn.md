## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `132` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Depressed mind. Inhibitory intervention. Stopping someone or something. Late procreation.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `197`, PDF p. `97` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

strenuous emotional problems, tasks of a creative woman, beginning emotional sorrow, birth problems of a woman, achievements of patience of a people, inhibited striving for goals of a woman, problems of the leadership of a people, and mature achievement of a woman

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: creative goals or activity encounter inhibition, burdens, or delay.
- psychology: depressed mood, emotional problems, and beginning sorrow are attributed source terms.
- body/health: late procreation and birth problems are historical claims, not reproductive assessments.
- social/relationship: a creative woman's tasks and problems of collective leadership are examples.
- events/manifestations: stopping activity, inhibited goal pursuit, patient achievements, and mature work are stated.
- conflicts/notes: patience and mature accomplishment coexist with inhibition; delay does not establish eventual success.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes stopping and inhibition, while ABC also names patient or mature accomplishment. These describe a range under difficulty without proving that restrictions always lead to mastery.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Moon/Zeus = Saturn, Saturn specifies the pair through inhibition or burden affecting creative effort and goals. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) names stopping or inhibitory intervention; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds emotional problems, difficult tasks, and inhibited striving, while retaining patient and mature accomplishments (printed p. 197, PDF p. 97). The entries' reproductive and depressed-mood examples remain historical associations. Their account of restraint does not establish either inevitable failure or eventual success.
