## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `252` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Others experience changes in their economic situation. Change in relation to the environment or to other people.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `402`, PDF p. `200` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

dissolution of depressions in the partner, restrictions and disappointments in the environment, sensitivity of a thorough partner, thoroughly disappointed partner, circulatory weakness of the partner, air resistance in the space, dissolution of congestion at a place, and water congestion in a place

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: changed economic or living conditions experienced through others or immediate surroundings.
- psychology: partner disappointment, sensitivity, and relief from depression are ABC examples.
- body/health: ABC's partner circulatory weakness and depression remain historical claims, not diagnoses.
- social/relationship: a partner and other people undergoing economic changes are the stated relationships.
- events/manifestations: local water congestion, release of congestion, and air resistance are listed.
- conflicts/notes: the examples include release as well as restriction; changed surroundings are not necessarily declining surroundings.

## Contradictions

Witte describes economic and relational changes without assigning a fixed direction. ABC includes both restrictions and dissolution of depression or congestion, so decline cannot stand for the whole range.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Admetos = Asc, Asc specifies the pair through changes encountered in other people or one's surroundings. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) names others' economic changes and altered relations with people or the environment. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds partner disappointment and sensitivity, but also dissolution of depression and local congestion, alongside water congestion and air resistance (printed p. 402, PDF p. 200). These examples leave room for release and restriction; the bodily terms remain historical source claims.
