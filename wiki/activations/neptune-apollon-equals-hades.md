## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `251` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Chemical science. To drain, to water. Ill vapors, gases, bad smelling matters.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `401`, PDF p. `199` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Dangerous metaphysical experiences, dangerous amounts of water, adverse spread of uncertainty, uncertainty through economic crime, hidden metaphysical knowledge, increased air pollution, and renunciation of future knowledge

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: chemical or water-related activity alongside adverse atmospheric and uncertainty examples.
- psychology: ABC names uncertainty without a broader personality account.
- body/health: ill vapors, gases, and increased air pollution are source examples, not evidence of exposure or illness.
- social/relationship: economic crime is ABC's stated social example.
- events/manifestations: chemical science, draining or watering, dangerous water quantities, and hidden metaphysical knowledge are listed.
- conflicts/notes: chemical science and drainage are not inherently corruption; adverse examples retain their specific settings.

## Contradictions

Witte's chemical and drainage activities are broader than its ill-vapor examples. ABC's dangerous water, uncertainty, and metaphysical themes do not establish a single toxic mechanism.

## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Apollon = Hades, Hades specifies the pair through chemical and water-related activity with adverse atmospheric examples. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) names chemical science, draining, watering, ill vapors, gases, and foul-smelling matter. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds dangerous water quantities, increased air pollution, uncertainty through economic crime, and hidden metaphysical knowledge (printed p. 401, PDF p. 199). These retain practical work as well as adverse conditions, without treating chemistry itself as corrupt or establishing an exposure.
