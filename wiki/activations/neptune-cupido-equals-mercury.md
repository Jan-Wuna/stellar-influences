## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `247` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Professional breach of promise (of marriage). Infidelity in married life. To think about the dissolution of a community, longing to leave it. Thoughts of sorrow and sadness about the dissolution of the family or about a shortage of housing .

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `392`, PDF p. `195` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Dissolution of a youth community, foreign art reports, tourism community (Fremden-Verkehrs-Gemeinschaft), conversation community in the air, youth community by or in water, talking about a shared future, and uncertain community among youth

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: thoughts or conversations about community, including separation and an uncertain shared future.
- psychology: longing to leave and sorrow about family dissolution are Witte's explicit themes.
- body/health: neither entry supplies a bodily or health claim.
- social/relationship: marital promises, infidelity, youth community, and conversation groups are stated.
- events/manifestations: thinking about dissolution, housing shortage, foreign art reports, and discussing a shared future are examples.
- conflicts/notes: the passages do not establish persistent rumination or make every community conversation a wish to separate.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes broken promises, infidelity, and thoughts of leaving. ABC's conversations and youth communities include a shared future as well as dissolution, without a necessary marital meaning.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Cupido = Mercury, Mercury specifies the pair through thoughts, reports, and conversations concerning a union or community. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes broken promises, infidelity, longing to leave, and sorrow over dissolution or housing shortage. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds youth-community dissolution, conversation groups, art reports, and discussion of a shared future (printed p. 392, PDF p. 195). These distinguish contemplation of separation from other collective communication; the entries do not establish constant rumination or make every conversation about leaving.
