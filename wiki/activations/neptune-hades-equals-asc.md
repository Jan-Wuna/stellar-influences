## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `248` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Others feel deceived and disappointed. To be deceived about the relations to the near surroundings. Sickness or death by water or dropsy.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `394`, PDF p. `196` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Dangerous place by the water, lack of air of the partner, air pollution at the location, dirty water nearby, weaknesses of the partner from the past, hidden water at the location, water shortage at the location, and water damage in the surroundings

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: uncertainty about others or nearby surroundings, with concrete environmental dangers.
- psychology: others' disappointment and deception about nearby relations are Witte's explicit themes.
- body/health: Witte's sickness, dropsy, and death claims are historical; no diagnosis follows from the local water examples.
- social/relationship: the partner, other people's feelings, and immediate surroundings are stated settings.
- events/manifestations: pollution, hidden water, water shortage, water damage, and a dangerous place by water are examples.
- conflicts/notes: the entries do not explain a general bodily wasting process or connect every environmental problem to deception.

## Contradictions

Witte combines interpersonal deception with bodily danger; ABC supplies specific local conditions. Neither says these must coincide in one event.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Hades = Asc, Asc specifies the pair through another person and the near surroundings. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes others feeling deceived or disappointed and uncertainty about nearby relations; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds a partner's past weaknesses and local pollution, hidden water, shortage, or damage (printed p. 394, PDF p. 196). These are concrete relational and environmental examples, without a stated common cause. Witte's severe bodily claims remain attributed historical wording rather than evidence of a condition in any particular person.
