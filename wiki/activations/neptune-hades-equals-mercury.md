## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `248` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Confused thinking, not to concentrate, to lose control of the summary, to be confused. Diseases of the brain. Raving madness. To think of sea voyages.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `394`, PDF p. `196` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Reports about air and water pollution, hindered water traffic, dangerous water movement, conversations about weaknesses of the past, youth crime on water, reports about water shortage, and talking about the future and the past

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: confused thought or difficulty concentrating, alongside reports and discussions of material problems.
- psychology: confusion and inability to concentrate are Witte's explicit descriptions; obsession is not stated.
- body/health: Witte's brain-disease and raving-madness terms remain historical wording, not a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: conversations, reports, and youth crime are specific communicative or group settings.
- events/manifestations: thinking of sea voyages, reporting pollution or shortage, and hindered water traffic are named.
- conflicts/notes: reporting an environmental problem does not establish that the reporter's thinking is disordered.

## Contradictions

Witte stresses confusion and also thoughts of sea voyages. ABC's reports may concern concrete environmental problems, without any claim that the speaker is confused.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Hades = Mercury, Mercury specifies the pair through thought, reports, and conversation. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes confusion and poor concentration, but also thoughts of sea voyages; [ABC](#abc-entry) gives pollution reports, discussions of past weaknesses, water shortage, and hindered water traffic (printed p. 394, PDF p. 196). These distinguish a difficulty in thinking from the subject being discussed. Environmental reporting need not be confused, and Witte's severe historical medical terminology does not establish a disorder in a particular person.
