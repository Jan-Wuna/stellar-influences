## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `248` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Deception, unpleasant things or want of clearness through female persons or in the public. Hydrocephalus. Death in small bodies of water. Cloudbursts.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `394`, PDF p. `196` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Troubled uncertain woman, the unknown and hidden aspects of a woman, lack of fluids of a woman, danger for a woman in water, sorrow through unclear feelings, disadvantages of a people from the past, and water damage for a people

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: unclear feelings or disappointment involving women or public life, with water danger as a distinct theme.
- psychology: sorrow through unclear feelings and an uncertain woman's distress are ABC descriptions.
- body/health: hydrocephalus, fluid deficiency, and death are historical source claims, not diagnoses or forecasts.
- social/relationship: women, the public, and a people affected by past disadvantages are the stated settings.
- events/manifestations: cloudbursts, water damage for a people, and danger in small bodies of water are examples.
- conflicts/notes: the gendered wording does not establish that women or the public are inherently deceptive or confused.

## Contradictions

Witte combines deception and public or female settings with severe bodily examples; ABC adds emotional uncertainty and collective disadvantages. Neither says those examples have to coincide.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Hades = Moon, Moon specifies the pair through unclear feelings, distress, or public and gendered circumstances. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes deception or lack of clarity involving women or the public; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds sorrow through unclear feelings and past disadvantages or water damage affecting a people (printed p. 394, PDF p. 196). These do not make all women or public relations deceptive. The fluid-deficiency, hydrocephalus, and death examples remain attributed historical claims, without an individual medical or accident prediction.
