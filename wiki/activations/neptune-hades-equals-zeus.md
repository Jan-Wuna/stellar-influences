## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `248` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

To drown oneself. Wasted energies or efforts. Deeds or acts of the past are of great hindrance for the future. Catastrophe for the navy. Burned by steam.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `395`, PDF p. `196` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Efforts against air pollution, feigning fire damage, dangerous aims in water, dangerous weaknesses from the past, criminal efforts on water, producing dirty water, and shaping the future from the past

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: effort affected by past actions, with waste, deception, or environmental activity among the examples.
- psychology: past weaknesses and intentional feigning of fire damage are stated without a universal motive.
- body/health: drowning and steam burns are historical Witte examples, not individual accident predictions.
- social/relationship: naval catastrophe and criminal activity on water are specific source settings.
- events/manifestations: futile effort, pollution control, producing dirty water, and shaping the future from the past are named.
- conflicts/notes: effort against pollution and producing pollution are different possibilities, not a single destructive process.

## Contradictions

Witte stresses wasted effort and past hindrance. ABC includes both pollution control and pollution production, so activity is not uniformly destructive or futile.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Hades = Zeus, Zeus specifies the pair through effort and how past actions bear on future activity. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) gives wasted energy and past deeds hindering the future; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds feigned fire damage, dangerous aims, and future-shaping from the past (printed p. 395, PDF p. 196). Efforts against air pollution differ from producing dirty water, preserving constructive and adverse directions. The severe naval, drowning, and steam-burn examples remain historical source claims, without a necessary destructive aftermath.
