## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `68` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

To drown. To feel disgusted with deception. To sustain losses through lies, lack of clarity or deception. To lead an evil life.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `81`, PDF p. `39` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Dangerous weakness of the soul, my service in the air, my sense for danger, my lack of intuition, my renunciation of metaphysics, my deficient breathing, my weakness for the old, my hidden uncertainty, and disadvantage through weakness of the soul

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: personal vulnerability, danger, and loss associated with uncertainty or deception.
- psychology: hidden uncertainty, deficient intuition, and an affinity for the old are stated.
- body/health: ABC names deficient breathing and dangerous weakness of the soul; Witte names drowning. These are historical claims, not individual diagnoses or forecasts.
- social/relationship: Witte associates losses with lies and deception rather than with a defined kind of person.
- events/manifestations: service in the air, renouncing metaphysics, and losses through lack of clarity are explicit examples.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's evil-life language is a historical moral judgement; ABC's final clause concerns weakness of soul, not a class of mentally weak people.

## Contradictions

The entries range from literal danger and breathing to uncertainty and loss through deception. They do not establish a single medical mechanism or justify applying Witte's moral judgement to a person.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/MC = Hades, uncertainty is linked to personal vulnerability and the cost of deception. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes losses through lies or lack of clarity; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds hidden uncertainty, deficient intuition, and a sense for danger (printed p. 81, PDF p. 39). Drowning, breathing deficiency, and weakness of the soul remain attributed historical claims. Witte's moral language does not warrant a general judgement of character.
