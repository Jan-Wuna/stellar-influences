## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `254` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

The spiritualized man. The man who knows himself. The man who follows the wrong faith, who advocates a destructive doctrine, who works for the destruction of existing doctrine or world concept.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `406`, PDF p. `202` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Mind body of the future, body in clear water, body weakness of a person, personal clarity through intuition, weak reason of a man, refined body culture, refinement of mind and body, and center for truths of metaphysics

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: spiritual self-understanding alongside adherence to or rejection of doctrine.
- psychology: self-knowledge, personal clarity, and weak reasoning are explicit.
- body/health: ABC's bodily weakness and refinement of mind and body are historical claims, not diagnoses.
- social/relationship: Witte's man-specific descriptions and destructive doctrine remain attributed.
- events/manifestations: a body in clear water and a center for metaphysical truths are source examples.
- conflicts/notes: psychology, devotion, porous identity, and a specific mode of study are not supplied by this entry.

## Contradictions

Witte pairs spiritualized self-knowledge with doctrine he judges wrong or destructive. ABC includes personal clarity and bodily or mental refinement alongside weak reason.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In Neptune/Poseidon = Sun, spiritual self-understanding and the beliefs one embodies are placed side by side. [Witte](#witte-entry) names knowing oneself and, separately, following or opposing doctrine; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds personal clarity through intuition, weak reason, and refinement of mind and body (printed p. 406, PDF p. 202). The source's gendered and doctrinal judgements remain attributed, without establishing a particular devotional or psychological role.
