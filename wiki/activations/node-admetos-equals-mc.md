## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `154` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

To feel attracted by oppressed people. Connections with lonely types of people who are avoided or who are ill reputed. To retain only a few connections. Connections which are separated through death. To feel in bonds or to long for freedom. Fetters.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `234`, PDF p. `116` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

limitation of my contacts, end of a soulful relationship, blocked soulful connection, my firm relationship, my restricted connections, my bond to the earth, my thorough connections, and spiritual oppression in a connection

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: one's own firm or limited ties, with attraction to oppressed or lonely people.
- psychology: feeling bound and longing for freedom are explicit.
- body/health: death is a historical separation example, not a forecast.
- social/relationship: Witte names few contacts and avoided or ill-reputed people; their reputation is attributed.
- events/manifestations: an ending of a soulful relationship and ties to the earth are ABC examples.
- conflicts/notes: few contacts alone do not establish distress, and reputation does not establish personal worth.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes bonds, loneliness and longing for freedom. ABC also names firm and thorough connections, so restriction is one condition rather than the whole meaning.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Node/Admetos = MC centers on one's own experience of connection and constraint. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes attraction to oppressed or lonely people, feeling bound and longing for freedom. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds restricted or ended soulful ties alongside firm, thorough connections (printed p. 234, PDF p. 116). The supported range includes attachment and limitation without treating a small circle as a defect. The reputation and death examples remain attributed historical claims rather than judgements or predictions.
