## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `154` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Close union with some women. End of the connection with the mother. Little connection with women or the public. The connection of people with their dead. Undeveloped mind. People close together in narrow rooms. Public under strong pressure.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `234`, PDF p. `116` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Restricted connection to a people, the end of a relationship with a woman, a firm connection to a woman, an emotional blockade within a connection, connection with a thorough woman, deepening the relationship to a woman, and deepening emotional relationships

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: close or limited ties involving women, family or a people.
- psychology: emotional blockage and deepened feeling both appear.
- body/health: Witte's undeveloped-mind judgement is historical wording, not a developmental diagnosis.
- social/relationship: close union, an ended maternal tie and limited public contact are stated alternatives.
- events/manifestations: crowding in narrow rooms and public pressure are particular examples.
- conflicts/notes: ties to the dead are source examples, not a forecast of bereavement.

## Contradictions

Witte combines close union, limited contact and collective pressure. ABC likewise includes firm or deepened ties as well as blockage and ending; they are not one necessary sequence.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Node/Admetos = Moon concerns closeness that may be firm, limited or emotionally blocked. [Witte](#witte-entry) names close union with some women and restricted public contact, while [ABC](#abc-entry) adds both deepened emotional relationships and a connection ending or encountering a feeling blockade (printed p. 234, PDF p. 116). Crowding and public pressure provide collective examples. The maternal, death-related and evaluative mental-language claims remain attributed rather than becoming diagnoses or predictions.
