## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. `153` (entry start; one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Poor but free. Many people in this situation.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. `233`, PDF p. `115` (entry).

#### ABC Entry

Successful connections in need, conveying dangerous knowledge, criminal trade connections, defective trade connections, connection to secret knowledge, many adverse connections, and conveying knowledge of the past

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: freedom or useful ties may coexist with poverty, need or disadvantage.
- psychology: no required motive for secret or historical knowledge is given.
- body/health: no distinct bodily claim is supplied.
- social/relationship: criminal trade is one particular example and does not characterize all poor or free people.
- events/manifestations: dangerous, secret or historical knowledge and defective trade ties are named.
- conflicts/notes: poverty is not equated with criminality, and freedom does not remove every disadvantage.

## Contradictions

Witte contrasts poverty with freedom. ABC adds successful ties in need alongside adverse or criminal trade; these examples must not become a judgement about poverty.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Node/Apollon = Hades concerns connections and knowledge under disadvantaged conditions. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes being poor yet free, with many sharing that situation. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds successful ties in need and knowledge of the past, beside defective trade, dangerous knowledge and particular criminal connections (printed p. 233, PDF p. 115). The passages allow freedom or useful connection amid hardship; they do not equate poverty with criminality or make all hidden knowledge dangerous.
