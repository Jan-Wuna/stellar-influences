## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 262 (one-based; exact row checked).

Bad things, sorrow and sadness are on the way. An evil change of the existing conditions. After a far reaching crisis an existing decadence predominates and leads to final ruin.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 419 (printed); PDF page: 208 (one-based; exact row checked).

Restriction of a criminal development, development made difficult by depression, thorough development from the past, deficient cyclical/circulatory development, adverse changes through depression, growing resistance to criminality.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: adverse or crisis-laden deep change, with resistance to criminal development also named.
- psychology: sorrow, sadness and the source's depressive development.
- body/health: ABC's Kreislauf wording can mean a cycle or circulation; it identifies no bodily mechanism or diagnosis; its depressive terms also remain historical source language.
- social/relationship: criminal development and resistance to criminality are alternative source expressions.
- events/manifestations: a far-reaching crisis, deteriorating conditions or development from the past.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's ruin language is severe and conditional in its wording; ABC also preserves restriction of crime and resistance to it.

## Contradictions

Witte's ruin language is severe and conditional in its wording; ABC also preserves restriction of crime and resistance to it.

## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies `Pluto/Admetos` through the adverse or deteriorating side of deep change. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) follows sorrow and worsening conditions into a far-reaching crisis whose existing decadence leads to ruin. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) includes difficult development and adverse changes through depression, but also restriction of criminal development and growing resistance to criminality. Those latter possibilities keep the passage from being reduced to unavoidable decline. Its evaluative and medical language remains attributed source doctrine.
