## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 225.

Serious ailment of the body. To be without work. To be under arrest. Losses of the people. Physical diseases that are not noticed in time.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 356; PDF page: 177.

Personal tasks from the past, secret tasks of a man, severe renunciation for the man, corporal perseverance in danger, lack of personal concentration, adverse problems for the man, and need of an old man

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Personal endurance under deprivation, danger, or burdens inherited from the past.
- Psychology: Poor concentration and severe renunciation accompany demanding personal tasks.
- Body/health: The Witte entry names serious or late-recognized illness; these are historical associations, not a diagnosis.
- Social/relationship: Unemployment, arrest, and the source's specifically male examples place restriction in a social setting.
- Events/manifestations: Difficult duties, loss of work, confinement, and perseverance in danger.
- Conflicts/notes: The ABC entry also describes endurance; hardship is not its only personal response.

## Contradictions

The entries emphasize different aspects of personal hardship. Witte's illness and confinement examples do not establish that every difficult duty in the ABC entry has those outcomes.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/Hades = Sun`, Sun specifies the Saturn/Hades axis through personal and bodily hardship: demanding duties, deprivation, and the effort to endure danger. The [ABC entry, printed p. 356](#abc-entry) combines tasks from the past and poor concentration with physical perseverance, so the picture includes how someone bears a burden as well as the burden itself.

The [Witte entry, PDF p. 225](#witte-entry) extends this to loss of work, arrest, and serious illness. Those are distinct historical examples, not inevitable outcomes; its medical statements and the ABC entry's male framing remain attributed to their sources.
