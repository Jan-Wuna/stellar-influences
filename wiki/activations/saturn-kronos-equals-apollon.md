## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: PDF p. 227.

To be very methodical and thorough. Expenses for schools or pertaining to education. Expenses of the state or of independent persons for scientific purposes.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Losses of leaders' reputations, spread of great pain, concentration on special knowledge, extensive tasks of authority, extensive state losses, multiplication of state problems, and important tasks of science.

Translation checked against the German scan: printed p. 361, PDF p. 179.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Thorough work and specialized knowledge meet the demands and costs of institutions.
- Psychology: Method, concentration, and attention to particular knowledge.
- Body/health: The ABC entry mentions great pain without identifying a bodily condition.
- Social/relationship: Authority, the state, schools, and independent persons frame the responsibilities.
- Events/manifestations: Educational or scientific expenditure, extensive official tasks, state losses, and reputation loss.
- Conflicts/notes: Spending on education or science is not itself the same as the ABC entry's adverse state losses.

## Contradictions

Witte stresses method and expenditure, while the ABC entry stresses burdens, knowledge, and loss. The entries do not establish whether a particular institutional expenditure is productive or damaging.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/Kronos = Apollon`, Apollon specifies the Saturn/Kronos axis through methodical work, specialized knowledge, and responsibilities on an institutional scale. The [Witte entry, PDF p. 227](#witte-entry) connects thoroughness with spending on schools and scientific purposes; the [ABC entry, printed p. 361](#abc-entry) adds concentration on knowledge and extensive official or scientific tasks.

The same ABC passage includes reputation loss, state losses, and multiplied problems. These keep the costs and burdens visible, without treating every educational expenditure or scientific responsibility as a failure.
