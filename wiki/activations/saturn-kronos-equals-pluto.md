## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: PDF p. 227.

To win or lose one’s independence. To retire from office through removal (fall) of the chief or overthrow of government.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Particularly patient growth, development of reputation losses, development of state problems, concentration on a particular development, change through great pain, loss of independent development, and developing important tasks.

Translation and complete paragraph checked against the German scan: printed p. 361, PDF p. 179. The opening clauses had been absorbed into the preceding Neptune paragraph in the extraction.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Changes in office, authority, or personal development alter independence.
- Psychology: Patient growth and focused development can coexist with painful change.
- Body/health: Great pain is named without a specific bodily condition.
- Social/relationship: Removal of a chief or government affects others' offices and independence.
- Events/manifestations: Gain or loss of independence, departure from office, evolving state problems, and reputation loss.
- Conflicts/notes: The scan restores the full Pluto paragraph from a bullet-only cache and a Neptune/Pluto boundary error.

## Contradictions

Witte explicitly allows both gaining and losing independence; the ABC entry stresses patient development and adverse changes. This preserves a range of outcomes rather than proving that change is uniformly damaging.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/Kronos = Pluto`, Pluto specifies the Saturn/Kronos axis through development and changes in authority that reshape independence. The [Witte entry, PDF p. 227](#witte-entry) allows independence to be gained or lost and describes leaving office when a chief or government falls; the verified [ABC entry, printed p. 361](#abc-entry) adds patient growth, painful change, and evolving official or reputational problems.

These passages support a changing relationship between individual development and institutional position. They preserve the possibility of gained independence alongside losses, without borrowing the neighboring Neptune passage or treating every development as an overthrow.
