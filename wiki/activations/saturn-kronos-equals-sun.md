## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: PDF p. 227.

Impoverishment of an independent person. The man who has grown old, gained many experiences in life. Days on which the authorities hinder and separate.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 360; PDF p. 179.

Tasks of a hierarchical centre, special patience of a person, serious authority person, great corporal pains, personal reputation loss, personal loss by the authority, loss of a state center, and important personal tasks

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Personal independence, status, and duties are tested by authority and experience over time.
- Psychology: Patience and seriousness of authority coexist with the burden of personal losses.
- Body/health: The ABC entry names bodily pain without identifying a condition.
- Social/relationship: Independent persons and authority figures meet official hindrance or separation.
- Events/manifestations: Impoverishment, reputation loss, state-center loss, and important personal or hierarchical tasks.
- Conflicts/notes: Witte also includes the experience of an older man; age is not presented solely as impoverishment.

## Contradictions

Experience and patience are distinct from the entries' losses. The passages do not establish that old age, independence, or authority necessarily brings poverty or bodily pain.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/Kronos = Sun`, Sun specifies the Saturn/Kronos axis through the person's independence, position, and demanding responsibilities. The [ABC entry, printed p. 360](#abc-entry) joins patience and important personal tasks with bodily pain, reputation loss, and losses through authority; the [Witte entry, PDF p. 227](#witte-entry) adds impoverishment and official hindrance.

Witte also names a man who has grown old and gained experience, preserving a meaning beyond loss alone. Its male framing and the bodily claims remain historical source examples, rather than universal consequences of age or authority.
