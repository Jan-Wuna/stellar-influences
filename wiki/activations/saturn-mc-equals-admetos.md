## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: PDF p. 66.

#### Witte Entry

To be crowded and hindered for a long time. To be in a dilemma. “I can and must wait”. To have great patience.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source reference: printed p. 77; PDF p. 37. Translation checked against the German row.

#### ABC Entry

Perseverance of a mature soul, limitation of my tasks, strengthening of my patience, my disturbed circulation (`gestörter Kreislauf`), my thorough concentration, my painful depressions, suppression of my pains, and deepening into soul-related tasks.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: prolonged hindrance met by patience and concentrated attention.
- psychology: endurance, a sense of dilemma, and deep absorption in limited tasks.
- body/health: ABC names painful depressions, suppressed pain, and disturbed `Kreislauf`; these remain attributed terms.
- social/relationship: Witte describes being crowded; neither entry specifies a particular relationship.
- events/manifestations: long delays, restricted tasks, waiting, and deepening concentration.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's `Kreislauf` is retained with its German wording; its precise bodily or cyclical sense is not developed here.

## Contradictions

- The entries join patience to restriction rather than promising that waiting resolves the dilemma.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/MC = Admetos`, Admetos specifies the Saturn/MC axis through prolonged restriction that demands waiting and sustained concentration. [Witte](#witte-entry) makes the dilemma explicit: one is crowded or hindered for a long time yet has great patience. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds narrowed tasks, strengthened patience, thorough concentration, and deeper engagement with soul-related tasks. Its painful depressions and suppressed pain describe the difficult side of that endurance; they do not establish a diagnosis.
