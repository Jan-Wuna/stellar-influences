## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: `231` (entry start).

One’s own behavior. The student or the educator. To be an example. To separate or differentiate mentally.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: `368`; PDF page: `183`.

mental and emotional disturbances, my patience and reason, my spiritual tasks, my spiritual maturity, my concentration on the truth, my problems with the truth, emotional burden through the public, and emotional pain through truth

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: One's own conduct, maturity and tasks in relation to truth and education.
- psychology: Patience, reason and concentration on truth alongside difficulty or pain in facing it.
- body/health: Mental and emotional disturbance is ABC's historical language, not a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: Student, educator and personal example are explicit roles; public life can feel burdensome.
- events/manifestations: Spiritual tasks, mental differentiation and emotional strain through truth.
- conflicts/notes: Being an example does not establish correctness; truth is described as both a focus and a source of difficulty.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes personal conduct and educational roles; ABC emphasizes personal maturity and the cost of truth. These are complementary settings without an explicit disagreement.
<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Saturn/Poseidon = MC`, MC specifies educational and spiritual concerns as one's own responsibility: conduct can become an example, whether one is learning or teaching, and one's outlook may differentiate from others ([Witte](#witte-entry)). [ABC](#abc-entry) places patience, reason and maturity beside concentration on truth and the pain it can bring. The unifying concern is how understanding is carried personally, including strain from public life; the historical disturbance language does not establish a diagnosis.
