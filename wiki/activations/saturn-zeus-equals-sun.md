## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: PDF page 226 (one-based).

Days on which one does not see any success from efforts. The man who quits his work or his workplace. Physical injuries or losses through steam power or fire.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 358; PDF page 178 (one-based).

Beginning of separation from a man, human leadership problems, personal efforts in old age, personal achievements of concentration, personal starting difficulties, problematic goal-setting by a man, and problems of a performance center.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal effort encounters starting difficulties, limited results or separation from work.
- psychology: Concentration and effort remain present alongside problematic goal-setting.
- body/health: Witte lists injuries through steam power or fire as historical source claims.
- social/relationship: Separation from a man or workplace; ABC includes leadership problems.
- events/manifestations: Unsuccessful efforts, departure from work and problems at a performance center.
- conflicts/notes: ABC retains personal concentration achievements; the formula does not establish inevitable failure or injury.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Witte foregrounds visible lack of success and departure; ABC includes sustained personal effort and concentration. The sources' gendered and injury statements remain attributed examples.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Saturn/Zeus = Sun`, the difficulty of producing results becomes personal: effort may meet a difficult start, problematic goals or separation from one's work. [Witte](#witte-entry) emphasizes efforts with no visible success and leaving a workplace; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds leadership problems while retaining concentration achievements and effort in old age. Its center of gravity is effort under personal strain, with the sources' fire injuries and gendered separation examples kept as historical particulars rather than predictions.
