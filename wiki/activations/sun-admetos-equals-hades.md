## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 120; PDF page 116 (one-based).

Lack of things of household. Death through exhaustion. An abused or worthless piece of property.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 169; PDF page 83 (one-based).

Depression due to bodily deficiency, restrictions due to bodily dirt, danger to bodily circulation, disadvantages due to physical resistance, criminal oppression of a person, and personal immersion in the past.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Deprivation, degraded property or bodily constraint, with a separate theme of immersion in the past.
- psychology: ABC attributes depression to bodily deficiency and names personal immersion in the past.
- body/health: Circulatory danger and Witte's death through exhaustion are historical claims, not diagnoses or predictions.
- social/relationship: Criminal oppression of a person in ABC.
- events/manifestations: Missing household necessities, abused or worthless property and restrictions involving bodily dirt.
- conflicts/notes: The severe bodily examples and the historical-interest clause are distinct; neither establishes the other.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Both entries include deprivation or damage, with different material and bodily examples. ABC's immersion in the past is an additional meaning and should not be erased by the severe examples.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Admetos = Hades`, the entries describe restriction through deprivation, degradation or oppression. [Witte](#witte-entry) gives household lack and abused or worthless property; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds bodily deficiency, dirt, physical resistance and criminal oppression. ABC also retains personal immersion in the past as a separate expression. The sources' depression, circulatory danger and death through exhaustion remain historical attributions, without turning this formula into a medical or mortality judgment.
