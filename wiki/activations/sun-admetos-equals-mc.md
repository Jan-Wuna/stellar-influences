## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 120; PDF page 116 (one-based).

Obstinate and steadfast. Waiting patiently. To retire into oneself. Great firmness or steadfastness of the body.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 168; PDF page 83 (one-based).

My bodily circulation, my personal resistance, my heavy body, my personal endurance, an inwardly blocked person, an inwardly firmly established person, an inwardly oppressed person, and a person's inner depth.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personally experienced endurance, firmness or inner blockage.
- psychology: Patience, obstinacy, inward retreat and depth, alongside oppression or blockage.
- body/health: Bodily firmness, heaviness and circulation are named without a specific diagnosis.
- social/relationship: The emphasis is personal; no particular relationship arrangement is established.
- events/manifestations: Waiting patiently, retiring into oneself and maintaining resistance.
- conflicts/notes: Inner firmness and inner blockage are different conditions; retreat is not automatically either one.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Both entries emphasize personal firmness and endurance. ABC retains inward blockage or oppression alongside inner depth, preventing patience from being treated as uniformly comfortable.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Admetos = MC`, firmness and endurance become a personal, inward experience. [Witte](#witte-entry) links steadfastness and obstinacy with patient waiting and withdrawal into oneself; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds personal resistance, bodily heaviness and inner depth, while also naming inward blockage or oppression. This supports a reading of sustained inward concentration or difficult immobility, with the distinction supplied by the entry's separate conditions rather than by assuming all withdrawal is constructive.
