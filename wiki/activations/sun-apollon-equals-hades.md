## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 119; PDF page 115 (one-based).

Refused or frustrated success. Celebrities of antiquity. Fading glory of the past. To rest on one’s laurels.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 167; PDF page 82 (one-based).

Old human knowledge, dangerous knowledge of a person, criminal experiences of a man, man with hidden knowledge, personal experiences of the past, many people in danger, and center for secret knowledge

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Past or frustrated recognition, and knowledge that is old, hidden or dangerous.
- psychology: Resting on earlier achievements is explicit in Witte.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is established by these entries.
- social/relationship: Celebrities of antiquity and many people in danger.
- events/manifestations: Fading glory, frustrated success, past experiences and a center for secret knowledge.
- conflicts/notes: Hidden knowledge does not establish research expertise or criminal character; these are distinct source examples.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Both entries contain a past-oriented theme. Witte emphasizes recognition and its decline; ABC adds secret or dangerous knowledge and experience, without asserting successful investigation.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Apollon = Hades`, recognition and experience turn toward what is past, concealed or frustrated. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes ancient celebrities, fading glory and resting on laurels, alongside refused success; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds old knowledge, past experience and secret or dangerous knowledge. Its criminal experiences and endangered people remain particular source examples. The passages support a relation to past or hidden experience, without establishing an accomplished researcher or investigator.
