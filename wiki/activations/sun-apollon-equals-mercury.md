## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 119; PDF page 115 (one-based).

To talk about one’s successes. Linguistic talent. Faculty of speech. Vainglorious. Successful orator or author. To brag.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 166; PDF page 82 (one-based).

Reports of a trade center, successful daily news, success in young years, conversations about personal freedom, crowds of people in traffic, personal experience in youth, and personal media experience.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Success expressed, reported or achieved through speech and communication.
- psychology: Linguistic talent and vainglory coexist in Witte's examples.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is established by these entries.
- social/relationship: Talking about one's achievements and conversations about personal freedom.
- events/manifestations: Successful speaking or authorship, commercial reports, media experience and youthful success.
- conflicts/notes: Talent and boasting are separate expressions; commercial reports do not alone establish commercial success.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

The entries overlap in success and communication. ABC adds youth, media and traffic settings; Witte retains both effective expression and self-glorification.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Apollon = Mercury`, success becomes a subject or result of communication. [Witte](#witte-entry) links linguistic talent and successful speaking or authorship with talking about one's achievements, vainglory and boasting; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds commercial reports, daily news, media experience and conversations about personal freedom. Its youthful success and crowded traffic are further settings. The entries support both effective expression and self-advertisement, without making either one the necessary character of every speaker.
