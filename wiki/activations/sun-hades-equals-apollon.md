## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 116; PDF page 112 (one-based).

Epidemics. Mass sickness. The science of the sick body. Illegal enterprises (business transactions).

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 161; PDF page 79 (one-based).

Success through personal deprivations, successful personal past, inadequate personal success, personal knowledge of the past, many people in need, science of the mystery of man, and center for the knowledge of the past

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Need or disadvantage at a broad scale, with knowledge and varied forms of personal success.
- psychology: No specific inner disposition is established by these entries.
- body/health: Witte's epidemics, mass sickness and study of the sick body are historical attributions.
- social/relationship: Many people in need in ABC.
- events/manifestations: Success through deprivation, knowledge of the past, a center for that knowledge and illegal transactions.
- conflicts/notes: Medical examples do not exhaust the entry; personal success and continuing need can coexist.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Witte emphasizes mass illness, bodily study and illegal business. ABC adds historical knowledge and success through deprivation alongside inadequate success; these are different settings rather than a single medical judgment.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Hades = Apollon`, the entries extend need or disadvantage into collective and knowledge-related settings. [Witte](#witte-entry) names mass illness, study of the sick body and illegal transactions; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds many people in need, knowledge of the past and success gained through personal deprivation, while also retaining inadequate success. The formula therefore includes inquiry and achievement as well as collective difficulty. The medical and criminal examples remain attributed source meanings, not judgments about a person or forecasts of disease.
