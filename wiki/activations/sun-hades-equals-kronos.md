## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Source locator: printed page 116; PDF page 112 (one-based).

Physical difficulties or restrictions through the state, officials or superiors. Pursued by misfortune through higher power. Leading among criminals. Crime sanctioned by the government.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Source locator: printed page 161; PDF page 79 (one-based).

Standing of a person from the past, danger through one's personal superior, great personal deprivation, great human need, dominance of male leadership, personal service to the state, state service personnel, and important service to people.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Need, deprivation or restriction involving authority, alongside service to people or the state.
- psychology: No specific inner disposition is established by these entries.
- body/health: Witte's bodily restrictions through authority remain historical source claims.
- social/relationship: Danger through a superior, state service and male leadership dominance.
- events/manifestations: Government-sanctioned crime, criminal leadership, great deprivation and a past person's standing.
- conflicts/notes: Important service remains explicit; authority is not uniformly characterized as bad.

## Contradictions And Comparison Notes

Witte emphasizes harmful authority and sanctioned crime. ABC also names important service and state personnel, which are distinct settings rather than evidence of criminality.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

For `Sun/Hades = Kronos`, the entries place deprivation, need or restriction in relation to authority. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes bodily restriction through the state or superiors and explicitly includes criminal leadership or government-sanctioned crime; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds danger through a superior, major deprivation and leadership dominance, but also important service to people and state service. Its past person's standing is a further historical theme. The formula retains both harmful authority and service without making every official or superior criminal.
