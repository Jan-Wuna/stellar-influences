## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Defects of the body react upon the soul and the reverse, which results in displeasure and unease. To feel disgusted.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Danger to body and soul, grief of my husband, criminal soul of a man, my personal secret, my bodily disadvantage, my personal lack, a person's spiritual past, and a man's hidden soul.

Source locator: printed p. 63, PDF p. 30, Sun/MC, Hades entry. The row ends “verborgene Seele des Mannes”; the following fiery-soul clauses belong to Zeus.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Personal unease, lack, or concealed distress.
- psychology: Disgust and displeasure in Witte; secrets and grief in ABC.
- body/health: Witte’s body/soul interaction is an attributed historical claim.
- social/relationship: ABC’s grief and criminal-soul examples remain attributed.
- events/manifestations: Experiencing lack, disadvantage, or a personal secret.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's criminal and bodily-danger examples remain attributed alternatives, not diagnoses or character judgments.

## Contradictions

The entries overlap on distress and deficiency without establishing a single cause. The ABC scan confirms bodily disadvantage and ends the Hades row at the man's hidden soul; the separate Zeus row begins with fiery soul.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Sun/MC = Hades`, Hades specifies the Sun/MC relation of body and inner life through unease, personal lack, or distress kept out of view. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) describes displeasure and disgust within its attributed account of body and soul affecting one another; the [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) adds grief, bodily disadvantage, lack, and a personal secret. These support a bounded interpretation of discomfort and concealed difficulty. They do not establish a diagnosis, criminal character, or suffering tied specifically to public status.
