## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Mental accomplishments during youth. Conceived and honorable thinking. Setting the standard. Commands that must be carried out. Decrees. Orders.

Source locator: PDF p. 103, Sun/Mercury, Kronos entry. Edition wording retained.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

A person's standing through writing, a person's great thoughts, a large youth center, an authority's personal opinion, a self-confident young man, a state news center, and superiority in personal conversation.

Source locator: printed p. 143, PDF p. 70, Sun/Mercury, Kronos entry. Translation checked against the complete German row.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Thought, writing or communication expressed with authority, standing or a standard-setting role.
- psychology: Self-confidence and great thoughts are ABC examples; no universal mental superiority is established.
- body/health: No bodily condition is supplied by these entries.
- social/relationship: An authority's opinion, commanding speech and standing gained through writing.
- events/manifestations: Youthful mental accomplishments, a large youth center, state news, decrees and orders.
- conflicts/notes: Authoritative status and the truth or value of an opinion remain separate questions; superiority is historical source language.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

`Sun/Mercury = Kronos` gives thought and communication an authoritative or standard-setting form. [Witte](#witte-entry) names youthful mental accomplishments, standards, decrees and orders; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds standing through writing, an authority's personal opinion and a state news center. Its self-confident young man and large youth center retain a distinct youthful setting. The common emphasis is the position or force carried by communication, without treating authority or the source's superiority language as proof that an opinion is true or inherently better.
