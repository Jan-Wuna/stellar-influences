## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Susceptible to serious epidemics. To be inclined to the use of narcotics. Destructive water or poison. The wrecking of an airship.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

man disappointed through hardship, endangered bodily fluids, bodily deficiencies and weaknesses, lack of personal sensitivity, disadvantages through foreign persons, personal sensitivity through privation, and hidden bodily poison

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: sensitivity or bodily vulnerability under conditions of deprivation and harmful exposure.
- psychology: disappointment through hardship; sensitivity developed through privation or a lack of personal sensitivity.
- body/health: the historical entries associate the formula with weakness, endangered fluids, poison, epidemics, and narcotics; these remain attributed claims.
- social/relationship: ABC names disadvantage through unfamiliar people, without establishing a universal relationship pattern.
- events/manifestations: Witte gives destructive water or poison and an airship wreck as concrete source examples.
- conflicts/notes: sensitivity through deprivation and lack of sensitivity are contrasting possibilities, not evidence that every person has both.

## Contradictions

- ABC presents both diminished sensitivity and sensitivity through privation. The entries do not explain when either applies. Their medical and narcotic associations cannot be converted into diagnoses or predictions.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies the Sun/Neptune axis through hardship and vulnerability: disappointment, weakness, and harmful substances give sensitivity a difficult material setting. [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) allows both reduced sensitivity and sensitivity developed through deprivation, so the throughline is the pressure of privation rather than a single psychological response. [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) extends the range to destructive water and poison; its epidemic and narcotic statements remain historical attributions, not health conclusions about a person.
