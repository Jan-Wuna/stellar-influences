## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Shared life meets obstacles and responsibilities, with community patience and maturation alongside separation or departure. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)
- psychology: ABC emphasizes patience and concentration on the community rather than a purely withdrawn personal attitude. [ABC](#abc-entry)
- body/health: Witte's surviving husband is a bereavement example; neither entry gives a specific bodily condition. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)
- social/relationship: Obstacles to living together, divorce and leaving a family or company are explicit, while ABC retains a community that can mature through its tasks. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)
- events/manifestations: Community work, difficulty maintaining shared life, departure from an organization and family separation are stated manifestations. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)
- conflicts/notes: Witte's severe separative examples coexist with ABC's patient and maturing community; the formula does not establish that every shared commitment ends. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)

## Contradictions

The emphasis differs materially: Witte foregrounds bereavement, divorce and departure, while ABC also allows patient communal life and maturation. These possibilities remain side by side.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

`Sun/Saturn = Cupido` places seriousness and difficulty within shared life. Responsibilities may demand patience and concentrated service to a community, but obstacles to living together can also result in leaving a family or company. Witte retains the surviving husband and divorced man as severe examples; ABC preserves the possibility that a community matures through its tasks. The common concern is the strain and sustained effort involved in belonging. [Witte](#witte-entry) · [ABC](#abc-entry)
