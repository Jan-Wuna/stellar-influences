## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `SUN + SUN`
- Source locator: PDF p. `99` (complete oriented entry; raw scan checked).

#### Witte Entry

Physical happiness. The fortunate man. The cheerful man. The rich or wealthy man. The well-being of the body. Good health. A tendency to be overweight. Corpulence. Day of fortune. Pay day.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Physical well-being and a cheerful or fortunate person are associated with wealth and benefit.
- psychology: Cheerfulness and physical happiness are named; self-acceptance is not separately stated.
- body/health: Good health, overweight, and corpulence are Witte's historical bodily associations, without a clinical assessment.
- social/relationship: The wealthy or fortunate person is described, but no specific relationship pattern is given.
- events/manifestations: Pay day and a day of fortune are explicit examples.
- conflicts/notes: These favorable and bodily associations form one source's range, not a guarantee of wealth or health.

## Contradictions

Only Witte is represented; cross-source agreement or disagreement cannot be assessed.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Sun/Sun = Jupiter`, Jupiter specifies the repeated Sun pair through physical happiness, cheerfulness, and favorable material circumstances. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 99) connects the fortunate or wealthy person with bodily well-being and pay day. Its health and body-size phrases are retained as attributed associations, while the interpretation remains about this range of benefit rather than a promise of prosperity or a theory of happiness in being oneself.
