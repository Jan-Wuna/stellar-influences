## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `SUN + VENUS`
- Source locator: PDF p. `104` (complete oriented entry; no printed folio visible; raw scan checked).

#### Witte Entry

There is no response to the own feeling of love. No ability to love.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Sonne + Venus`
- Source locator: printed p. `145`, PDF p. `71` (complete oriented entry; raw scan checked).

#### ABC Entry

Limitation of human joy, restriction of bodily love, the end of a harmonious day, strengthening of personal harmony, suppression of a man's love, deepening of personal affection, and goodwill of a steady person.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Love and harmony meet restriction, lack of response or a deepening and stabilizing emphasis.
- psychology: Limited joy and strengthened harmony are different ABC possibilities.
- body/health: Restriction of bodily love is stated; no medical diagnosis is supplied.
- social/relationship: Unreturned love and suppressed affection coexist with deepening affection and goodwill.
- events/manifestations: The end of a harmonious day is an ABC example; the entry supplies no timing calculation.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's no ability to love is an attributed absolute description, while ABC also permits affection to deepen.

## Contradictions

Witte gives only nonresponse and inability to love; ABC also names strengthened harmony, deepened affection and goodwill. These emphases remain distinct rather than being reduced to a single inevitable outcome.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Sun/Venus = Admetos`, Admetos specifies restriction within love, joy and harmony. [Witte](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 104) describes love receiving no response and uses the absolute phrase 'No ability to love.' [ABC](#abc-entry) (printed p. 145) also includes restricted or suppressed love, but adds strengthened harmony, deepened personal affection and goodwill from a steady person. Together the entries support both constraint and a more settled depth of affection as distinct possibilities. Witte's wording remains an attributed description rather than a universal judgment of a person's capacity to love.
