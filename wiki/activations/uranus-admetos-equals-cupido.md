## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 241.

#### Witte Entry

Sudden break-off with a community, club or family. To be shunned by an association. To be suddenly alone or lonesome.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 385; PDF p. 191.

#### ABC Entry

Consolidation through a shared rhythm, shared deepening into technical matters, thorough reforms for the community, a suddenly depressed community, tensions in a community through restrictions, surprising stabilization of a community.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: A community bond may be broken, restricted, consolidated or stabilized.
- psychology: Loneliness in Witte and a depressed community in ABC remain distinct emotional descriptions.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is supplied; collective depression is attributed source wording.
- social/relationship: Family, club or association relationships face separation, exclusion or shared consolidation.
- events/manifestations: A sudden break-off or shunning contrasts with community reform and stabilization.
- conflicts/notes: The two entries differ materially between social separation and stabilization; neither can replace the other.

## Contradictions

Witte describes exclusion and sudden loneliness. ABC also describes community restrictions and depression, but explicitly includes shared rhythm, thorough reform and stabilization. This is a material range of outcomes; the sources do not explain when separation or stabilization applies.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Admetos = Cupido`, Cupido specifies interruption or consolidation within a shared bond. [Witte](#witte-entry) emphasizes a break with family or an association and the loneliness of being shunned. [ABC](#abc-entry) retains tension and restriction but also describes shared rhythm, technical study, community reform and unexpected stabilization. A common bond may therefore be disrupted or made firmer; stabilization must remain visible beside the more severe separation claim.
