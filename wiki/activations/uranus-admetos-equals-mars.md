## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 241.

#### Witte Entry

Surprising break-off of an activity. Terror. Suppression.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 384; PDF p. 191.

#### ABC Entry

Endurance in rhythmic work, a dynamic cycle of activity, a consolidated work rhythm, thorough technical work, new stabilizing activity, work with new primordial matter, sudden restrictions in work, suppression of restlessness while creating.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: Activity abruptly interrupted or made steady through rhythmic, stabilizing work.
- psychology: Witte names terror; ABC also describes endurance and suppression of restlessness.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is stated.
- social/relationship: Suppression appears in Witte without a specified relationship or perpetrator.
- events/manifestations: Work can break off unexpectedly, while technical work and new stabilizing activity are also represented.
- conflicts/notes: Interruption and sustained work are materially different emphases; neither should erase the other.

## Contradictions

Witte emphasizes interruption, terror and suppression. ABC instead includes endurance, consolidated rhythm and stabilizing work alongside sudden restrictions. The entries do not establish a sequence in which terror produces productive endurance.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Admetos = Mars`, Mars specifies interruption or consolidation through activity itself. [Witte](#witte-entry) gives an unexpected break-off, terror and suppression. [ABC](#abc-entry) preserves restrictions in work but also describes endurance, a firm rhythm, technical work and stabilizing activity. This formula therefore retains both halted activity and work made steadier, without turning either into a necessary result of the other.
