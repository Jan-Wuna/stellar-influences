## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 241.

#### Witte Entry

Persons who are inclined to sudden rupture-like separations. Exciting difficult separations for women. Exciting or shaking events in public.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 384; PDF p. 191.

#### ABC Entry

Blocked emotional impulses, an end to unrest among the people, an excited depressed woman, a nervous woman in a traffic jam, a suddenly depressed woman, consolidation of excited feelings, sudden restrictions on a people, sudden freezing of feelings, surprising deepening of feeling.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: Emotional or collective interruption, with feelings that may be blocked, consolidated or deepened.
- psychology: Excitement, depression, emotional freezing and deeper feeling are ABC associations.
- body/health: Depression wording remains attributed without a diagnosis; Witte gives no distinct bodily condition.
- social/relationship: Difficult separations for women and restrictions affecting a people are historical source examples.
- events/manifestations: Publicly shaking events, an end to public unrest and sudden restriction or separation.
- conflicts/notes: Gendered claims remain attributed; Gefühle means feelings, not the former fleas translation.

## Contradictions

Both entries include disruption, but ABC also describes the end of unrest and emotional consolidation or deepening. These are different possibilities, without an established sequence or a universal claim about women.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Admetos = Moon`, Moon specifies interruption through feelings and public unrest. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes abrupt separations and events that shake the public; [ABC](#abc-entry) adds blocked or suddenly frozen feelings, while also preserving emotional consolidation, surprising depth and an end to unrest. The entries' examples of depressed or excited women remain historical, attributed illustrations rather than universal gender traits.
