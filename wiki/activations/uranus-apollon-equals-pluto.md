## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 240.

#### Witte Entry

The beginning of a new development with a very small beginning which grows in a short time into something very large and causes great tension. To be tense over the development of things.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 383; PDF p. 190.

#### ABC Entry

Disquieting changes in trade, successful new development, successful technical development, evolutionary unfolding of knowledge, developing new knowledge, developing technical knowledge, much unrest through changes, transformations through new knowledge.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: A small beginning rapidly grows into a large development with tension.
- psychology: Tension over development and unrest through change.
- body/health: No specific bodily condition is stated.
- social/relationship: Trade changes form one explicit social setting.
- events/manifestations: New and technical development, knowledge unfolding and change through new knowledge.
- conflicts/notes: The complete eight-clause Pluto row begins with trade changes; its opening was formerly absorbed into Neptune.

## Contradictions

Both entries associate development with tension or unrest. ABC also names successful new and technical development, but does not make every change successful or specify its final size. The corrected row boundary preserves these claims only for Pluto.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Apollon = Pluto`, Pluto specifies expansion as a development that can grow rapidly from a small beginning. [Witte](#witte-entry) emphasizes the great tension that growth causes. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds new and technical development, knowledge unfolding and transformations through learning, while retaining disquieting trade changes and unrest. Growth and discovery are the shared process; their success does not erase the strain.
