## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 240.

#### Witte Entry

To be depressed and pessimistic through being disappointed. Expectations, hopes and wishes must be throttled. Disappointments, buried hopes. Failures in predicting events.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 383; PDF p. 190.

#### ABC Entry

Concentration on new knowledge, nervousness through increased losses, a sudden increase in tasks, sudden experiences of pain, problematic impulses toward freedom, disturbances and unrest in trade, surprising loss of freedom, increasing technical problems.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: Expectation frustrated by disappointment, added burdens or lost freedom.
- psychology: Pessimism, depressed feeling and nervousness through increased losses.
- body/health: Pain and depression are attributed source associations, without a diagnosis.
- social/relationship: Commercial disturbance and restrictions of freedom.
- events/manifestations: Added tasks, technical problems and failures in predicting events.
- conflicts/notes: Increased losses replace the former fatal-loss wording; no death claim belongs to this ABC row.

## Contradictions

Witte gives disappointment and buried hopes, including failures of prediction. ABC adds concentration on knowledge alongside increased tasks, losses and technical problems. Concentration is retained without being presented as a cure for the difficulties.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Apollon = Saturn`, Saturn specifies expanding expectation through disappointment or constraint. [Witte](#witte-entry) describes hopes that must be reduced and failures in prediction. [ABC](#abc-entry) adds increased losses, tasks, commercial disturbance and loss of freedom, while retaining concentration on new knowledge. The difficult range concerns frustrated expectation and accumulating burdens; its pain and depression terms remain attributed.
