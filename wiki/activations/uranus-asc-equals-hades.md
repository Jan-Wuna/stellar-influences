## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 88.

#### Witte Entry

Lowness of others. Danger of assassination. To be accessory to a killing.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 117; PDF p. 57.

#### ABC Entry

Excitement about a dirty place, danger at the location through technology, a criminal event at the location, deficient environmental technology, disadvantageous reforms at the location, sudden grief with the partner, surprising distress in the environment, hidden unrest at the location.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).

- core meaning: Troubling events involving others or the immediate surroundings.
- psychology: Excitement, grief and hidden unrest.
- body/health: Witte's assassination/killing examples remain severe attributed claims, without a personal prognosis.
- social/relationship: Base acts by others, a partner's grief and criminal involvement appear.
- events/manifestations: Danger through technology, deficient environmental technology and disadvantageous reforms.
- conflicts/notes: Reforms and distress replace the former refutations and garbled distress wording.

## Contradictions

Witte gives extreme criminal examples. ABC also includes crime and technological danger, but ranges through deficient technology, harmful reforms, grief and hidden unrest. The broader local difficulties do not establish an assassination or personal criminal responsibility.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Asc = Hades`, Hades specifies unexpected interaction through troubling acts or conditions nearby. [ABC](#abc-entry) includes deficient technology, disadvantageous reforms, grief with a partner and hidden unrest. [Witte](#witte-entry) supplies the much more severe examples of assassination danger and being accessory to a killing. Those historical associations remain attributed possibilities, without turning every local difficulty into a violent event.
