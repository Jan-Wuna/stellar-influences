## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 237.

#### Witte Entry

Damage, rip which cannot be compensated, where one cannot make restitution. Dissension which cannot be bridged over. Irreconcilable enmity. Total destruction.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 377; PDF p. 187.

#### ABC Entry

Depression through sudden lack, consolidation through surprising renunciation, sorrow through sudden restriction, new disadvantages through a strike, stabilizing impulses from the past, outdated rotation technology.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 237) and [ABC](#abc-entry) (printed p. 377; PDF p. 187).

- core meaning: Sudden lack or restriction is experienced as damage, division or a need to consolidate.
- psychology: ABC names depression and sorrow; Witte describes irreconcilable enmity, without establishing a diagnosis.
- body/health: Depression is an attributed historical term, not a clinical conclusion; destruction is not specified as a bodily condition.
- social/relationship: Witte's unbridgeable dissension and inability to make restitution describe a severe rupture.
- events/manifestations: A strike, stabilizing impulses from the past and outdated rotation technology are further examples.
- conflicts/notes: Stabilization through renunciation remains distinct from irreparable damage; not every restriction is total destruction.

## Contradictions

Witte stresses damage that cannot be repaired, while ABC also permits consolidation and stabilization. These materially different possibilities must remain side by side.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Admetos specifies Uranus/Hades through restriction or lack that makes a rupture difficult to repair. [Witte](#witte-entry) gives the severe form as uncompensated damage and irreconcilable dissension; [ABC](#abc-entry) also allows consolidation through renunciation and stabilizing impulses from the past. Its sorrow and depression language describes an attributed response to deprivation, without establishing a clinical condition. Outdated rotation technology remains a separate concrete example.
