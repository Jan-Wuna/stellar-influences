## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 237.

#### Witte Entry

Many are vile. To suffer vile acts with many. Discoveries in historical science. Astrology which is sold at fairs in dime and quarter magazines. Pseudo science.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 377; PDF p. 187.

#### ABC Entry

Successful impulses from the past, trade in technical secrets, new secret knowledge, new knowledge about the past, sudden expansion of need, extensive technical deficiencies, experiencing increased danger.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 237) and [ABC](#abc-entry) (printed p. 377; PDF p. 187).

- core meaning: Knowledge of the past or technical secrets expands alongside possible collective hardship.
- psychology: The entries describe knowledge and experience, without establishing a fixed individual temperament.
- body/health: Increased danger is unspecified and does not establish a bodily condition or diagnosis.
- social/relationship: Witte describes many suffering harmful acts together.
- events/manifestations: Historical discoveries, trade in technical secrets and extensive technical deficiencies are explicit.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's criticism of popular astrology as pseudoscience is a source judgment, distinct from historical discovery.

## Contradictions

Historical discovery and successful past impulses coexist with criticism of pseudoscience and expanding need. Neither establishes that all knowledge of the past is reliable or harmful.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Apollon specifies Uranus/Hades through the reach of knowledge and experience concerning the past. Historical discoveries, technical secrets and new knowledge can yield successful impulses, while extensive deficiencies or expanding need give the same field a difficult collective scale. ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry).) Witte separately criticizes some popular astrology as pseudoscience and describes shared suffering from harmful acts; those judgments and severe examples remain attributed rather than defining every historical inquiry.
