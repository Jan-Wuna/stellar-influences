## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 237.

#### Witte Entry

Persons of low class or rank. Mean deeds of women. Grave ending, long agony or assassination of a woman. The hour of the murder. Widespread dying.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 376; PDF p. 187.

#### ABC Entry

A troubled people in need, agitation over emotional sorrow, dangerous events for a people, disadvantages through emotional excitement, tension through a lack of women, sudden lack for a woman, unrest over the distress of a people, outdated technology of a people.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 237) and [ABC](#abc-entry) (printed p. 376; PDF p. 187).

- core meaning: Collective need or emotional distress is experienced with agitation and danger.
- psychology: Emotional sorrow and disadvantage through emotional excitement are explicit, without establishing a disorder.
- body/health: Witte's agony, assassination and widespread dying are historical correspondences, not a prognosis.
- social/relationship: Female-person, people's-community and class/rank examples retain their historical limitations.
- events/manifestations: A people's outdated technology and unrest over collective distress are concrete examples.
- conflicts/notes: Class and gender labels are not evidence of moral character; harm received and harmful acts are different roles.

## Contradictions

Witte's class-based judgments and gendered harm examples must not be generalized. ABC's emotional and technical difficulties do not establish assassination or widespread death.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Moon specifies Uranus/Hades through a troubled emotional or collective condition: sorrow, agitation and need affect a people or a woman's circumstances in [ABC](#abc-entry). Outdated technology supplies a separate practical difficulty. [Witte](#witte-entry) adds severe historical examples involving agony, assassination and widespread death, together with class and gender judgments. Those remain attributed claims, while the common emotional theme does not establish a person's moral character or predict a fatal event.
