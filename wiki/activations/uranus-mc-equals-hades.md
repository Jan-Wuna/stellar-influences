## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: PDF p. 67.

#### Witte Entry

Excitement through ugly notions. To commit misdeeds. To execute plans for murder.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source locator: printed p. 79; PDF p. 38.

#### ABC Entry

An exciting minute of danger, agitation of a distressed soul, dangerous tensions of the psyche, my sudden disadvantage, my surprising renunciation, my restless past, my hidden tensions, my renunciation of technology.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 67), [ABC](#abc-entry) (printed p. 79; PDF p. 38).

- core meaning: Personal agitation amid disadvantage or harmful notions. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
- psychology: Hidden tension and a troubled relation to the past. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
- body/health: ABC psychological tension does not establish a medical diagnosis. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
- social/relationship: Witte's harmful conduct examples involve effects on others. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
- events/manifestations: Sudden disadvantage, renunciation, and the source's severe misdeed examples. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
- conflicts/notes: Personal distress and technical renunciation remain distinct from Witte's severe harmful-act examples; the entry does not establish moral character.

## Contradictions

Distress, hidden tension and renunciation do not by themselves establish the harmful conduct described by Witte. The technical theme and the person's restless past remain visible beside those severe examples.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Hades specifies Uranus/MC through agitation connected with harmful notions, hidden tension, or sudden disadvantage. The ABC entry places renunciation and a troubled past beside this inward unrest. Witte includes misdeeds and plans for murder as extreme expressions; these remain attributed historical examples and do not identify a person's conduct or character from the formula alone. ([Witte entry](#witte-entry); [ABC entry](#abc-entry).)
