#### Witte Entry

The will. Sudden fire. Discharge of firearms. To fire, to splinter. Fire explosion. Bolt of lightning. Sudden outbreak of war. Electric motor. Dynamo. Fatherhood. Explosions. Danger from fire. Controlled forces. Laws of nature. Sudden acts which cannot be suppressed. To explode, to ignite. Surprising events happening by natural force.

## Comparative Schema

Evidence: [Witte](#witte-entry).

- core meaning: Sudden ignition or an act of will releasing force.
- psychology: Will and an impulse that cannot readily be suppressed.
- body/health: Witte includes fatherhood, without establishing a medical claim.
- social/relationship: War and firearms are severe social examples in the source.
- events/manifestations: Fire, electric motors, dynamos and natural-force events.
- conflicts/notes: Fatherhood is preserved as a separate named association, without a supplied mechanism.

## Contradictions

Only Witte is represented. Controlled forces and acts described as unsuppressible are different expressions, not a demonstrated contradiction. The entry does not explain how fatherhood relates to its ignition imagery.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Uranus/Uranus = Zeus`, Zeus specifies sudden stimulation as ignition or a forceful act: something is fired, set in motion or expressed with compelling will. [Witte](#witte-entry) includes electric motors and dynamos alongside fire, firearms and natural forces. Fatherhood is also named but not explained; it remains a separate source association, while war and fire danger are attributed extremes rather than forecasts.
