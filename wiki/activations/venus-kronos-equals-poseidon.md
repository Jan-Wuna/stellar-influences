## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Venus + Kronos`
- Source reference: PDF page `185` (one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Noble, honorable disposition or world conception. Love for one's fellow-men. Good deeds motivated by conviction. Preference for high spiritual things or matters. High conception of morals and moral behavior.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Venus + Kronos`
- Source reference: printed page `289`; PDF page `143` (one-based).

#### ABC Entry

Special clarity through love, special love of truth, a great idea of peace, great preference for clarity, art in the cultural state, love of spiritual hierarchy, independent artists' ideas, spiritual priority of the female principle, and state peace culture.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Benevolence guided by conviction, moral ideals, truth, and clarity.
- Psychology: An honorable outlook and preference for spiritual matters, truth, or clarity.
- Body/health: Neither activation entry supplies a bodily or health claim.
- Social/relationship: Love for fellow human beings and love of spiritual hierarchy.
- Events/manifestations: Good deeds motivated by conviction, ideas of peace, cultural art, and independent artistic ideas.
- Conflicts/notes: Claims to moral nobility and spiritual priority of the female principle are source interpretations, not verified judgments.

## Contradictions

- Claims to moral nobility and spiritual priority of the female principle are source interpretations, not verified judgments.

## Derived Synthesis

`Venus/Kronos = Poseidon` gives benevolence a guiding conviction: love for other people becomes good deeds, an honorable outlook, and concern for moral conduct in [Witte's entry](#witte-entry). The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) joins love to truth, clarity, and an idea of peace, with cultural art and independent artists' ideas as further expressions. These entries describe ideals that can inform conduct. Their assertions of noble motives, spiritual hierarchy, and priority of the female principle retain the sources' ethical and gendered framing; they do not establish anyone's actual moral character.
