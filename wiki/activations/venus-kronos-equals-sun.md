## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Venus + Kronos`
- Source reference: PDF page `185` (one-based).

#### Witte Entry

Readiness for charitable activities. To receive the Nobel Peace Prize. To receive benevolence from the state or from superiors.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Venus + Kronos`
- Source reference: printed page `288`; PDF page `143` (one-based).

#### ABC Entry

An especially beautiful day, the man as an authority in love, dominance of bodily love, a great art center, personal harmony with hierarchy, a state peace center, preference for people of the state, and a person important for peace.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Personal readiness for charity and goodwill received from the state or superiors.
- Psychology: Willingness to help and personal harmony with hierarchy.
- Body/health: Bodily love is explicit in the ABC; no health condition is stated.
- Social/relationship: Benevolence from authorities and a person important to peace.
- Events/manifestations: Charitable activity, an art center, or a state peace center; Witte gives the Nobel Peace Prize as an example of recognition.
- Conflicts/notes: The Nobel Peace Prize is an illustrative historical claim, and the ABC's male authority in love is gendered source language.

## Contradictions

- The Nobel Peace Prize is an illustrative historical claim, and the ABC's male authority in love is gendered source language.

## Derived Synthesis

`Venus/Kronos = Sun` gives benevolence a personal expression in readiness for charity and in receiving goodwill from authorities or the state. [Witte](#witte-entry) offers the Nobel Peace Prize as a conspicuous example of recognition; that example does not predict an award. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds personal harmony with hierarchy, a person important to peace, and centers of art or state peace. Its emphasis on bodily love and the man as an authority in love is a distinct historical gendered theme. Helping, receiving favor, and being associated with public or artistic standing remain connected but separate possibilities.
