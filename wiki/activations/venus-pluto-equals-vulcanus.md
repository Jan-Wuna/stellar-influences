## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

To be very struck with an occurrence or experience. Great sympathy. Mighty longing for love. A powerful affection leads to politeness and consideration.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

- Source heading: `Venus + Pluto`
- Source reference: printed page `281`; PDF page `139` (one-based).

#### ABC Entry

Unfolding the strength of love, peaceful development of power, developing harmonious strength, wishing for a change of power, preference for energy transformation, transformation of the energy of love, growing artistic influence, growing power for peace, and growing strength of love.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Growth in the strength or influence of love and sympathy.
- psychology: Mighty longing for love and being strongly affected by an experience.
- body/health: No bodily or health claim is supplied.
- social/relationship: Powerful affection expressed through politeness and consideration.
- events/manifestations: Growing love power and peaceful or harmonious development of strength.
- conflicts/notes: ABC's energy-transformation wording remains source language and does not establish a physical mechanism.

## Contradictions

Strong feeling and considerate behavior are explicitly connected in Witte. The entries do not supply a direct contrary outcome, but their favorable wording does not establish that all force is benevolent.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Venus/Pluto = Vulcanus`, Vulcanus specifies Venus/Pluto development through the growing strength of affection or its effect on a person. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) connects strong sympathy and longing with politeness and consideration. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds unfolding love strength, peaceful or harmonious development of power, and growing artistic influence. Its energy-transformation phrases remain attributed rather than a physical explanation. The supported pattern is strong affection or influence developing in expression, with considerate behavior specifically stated by Witte.
