## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- Source reference: one-based PDF p. 188, Venus + Vulcanus, Node row; actual glyph and neighboring rows checked.

A union resulting from or caused by a great longing or passion.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. 294; one-based PDF p. 146, Venus/Vulcanus, Node row; complete row and adjacent glyph boundaries checked.

Editorial translation, checked against the scan: Influential love relationships, preferring influential connections, force or violence in a love relationship, harmonious relationships to power, combining art and power, powerful harmony in a relationship, beauty connected with power, and strong love connections.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Strong attraction forming bonds that carry influence, harmony or power.
- psychology: Longing or passion directed toward union; preference for influential connections.
- body/health: No bodily or medical condition is supplied.
- social/relationship: Strong love connections and harmony, with force or violence also explicit in ABC.
- events/manifestations: Combining art and power, and connecting beauty with power.
- conflicts/notes: Kunst und Macht verbinden means combining art and power. The Gewalt clause preserves force or violence without deciding every bond's character.

## Contradictions

Harmony and force or violence are separate stated possibilities. Neither makes every passionate union harmonious or coercive.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Venus/Vulcanus = Node`, strong longing or passion takes the form of a bond, as the [Witte entry](#witte-entry) states directly. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) extends the relationship's influence through strong love connections, harmony, beauty and power, including the combination of art and power and a preference for influential connections. Force or violence in a love relationship remains a separate clause. The strength of a bond alone therefore does not determine whether its influence is harmonious or coercive.
