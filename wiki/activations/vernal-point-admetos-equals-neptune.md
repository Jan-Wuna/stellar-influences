## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 53 (one-based).

To make solid. Solidification. To stop a dissolution or a putrefaction. Conservation. To embalm. To mummify. To preserve. Secret resistance.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 55; PDF page: 26 (one-based spread).

dissolution of public restrictions, depressions of a deceived public, public water restrictions, weak resistance in public life, water cycle in public, and uncertainty through suppression in public

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Preservation or secret resistance, alongside dissolution and uncertainty in public restrictions.
- psychology: Depression of a deceived public and uncertainty through suppression are stated.
- body/health: Embalming and mummification are historical preservation examples, not health guidance.
- social/relationship: Weak or secret resistance in public life.
- events/manifestations: Solidification, conservation, public water limits, and water circulation.
- conflicts/notes: The objects differ: Witte stops dissolution of matter; ABC dissolves public restrictions.

## Contradictions

Stopping dissolution and dissolving restrictions use opposite verbs with different objects. Both must remain visible; the entries do not establish a single universal direction of change.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/Admetos = Neptune`, Neptune specifies the pair through preservation, hidden resistance, or uncertainty about public limits. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) emphasizes making solid and preventing decay, with embalming and mummification as concrete examples. The [ABC entry](#abc-entry) instead allows public restrictions to dissolve, while also naming weak resistance, water limits, and uncertainty under suppression. These different objects prevent a simple equation of the formula with either dissolution or preservation alone.
