## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 43 (one-based).

Fortune in misfortune. Secret international financial transactions. Hidden or secret public money. Past public law.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 35; PDF page: 16 (one-based spread).

Success in public service, lack of money in public, criminal advantages in public, legal disadvantages in public, dirty money in public, past successes in public, and renunciation of public funds

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Public advantage amid deprivation, concealment, or compromised finance.
- psychology: Fortune in misfortune is explicit; no separate mental trait is supplied.
- body/health: No bodily or health claim is supplied.
- social/relationship: Public service is a favorable example alongside legal disadvantage.
- events/manifestations: Secret international transactions, hidden funds, money shortage, and past success.
- conflicts/notes: Criminal advantage and dirty money are particular source examples, not definitions of public service.

## Contradictions

Successful public service and past achievements coexist with money shortages, criminal advantage, and renunciation. Hidden finance is not automatically identified as criminal.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/Jupiter = Hades`, public fortune is considered where money or advantage is concealed, scarce, inherited from the past, or obtained under adverse conditions. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) calls this fortune in misfortune and names secret international finance. The restored [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds successful public service and past success beside shortages, legal disadvantage, dirty money, and relinquished funds. Benefit and deprivation therefore remain side by side, with wrongdoing confined to the examples that explicitly name it.
