## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

The body in public. The behavior.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. `18`, one-based PDF p. `8`, complete Sun row.

German passage transcribed from the scan:

> Beseelter Mensch im öffentlichen Leben, beseeltes Zentrum in der Öffentlichkeit, mein Mann in der Öffentlichkeit, meine Person in der Öffentlichkeit, Menschen-Seelen in der Öffentlichkeit, mein Leben in der Öffentlichkeit

Editorial translation: A soulful person in public life; a soulful center in public; my husband in public; my person in public; human souls in public; my life in public.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: One's embodied presence and behavior in public.
- Psychology: Behavior is named without motives or a character theory.
- Body/health: Witte names the body, without a health condition.
- Social/relationship: ABC includes a husband in public as a particular example.
- Events/manifestations: Personal life and presence in public.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC uses soulful person/center and human-souls vocabulary without establishing blessing, moral merit or an exclusive male rule.

## Contradictions

Witte's bodily focus and ABC's personal-life examples are compatible. The soul vocabulary does not establish a contrast between body and spirit; the male example is not an exclusive gender rule.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/MC = Sun`, the Sun specifies one's public position through the actual person present and their behavior. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) (PDF p. 35) names the body in public; the personal-life clauses of the complete [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) (printed p. 18; PDF p. 8) make the focus one's own public presence, with a husband as one attributed example. Neither entry supplies a medical or moral judgment.
