## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

General rise. Changes in the government.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. `43`, one-based PDF p. `20`, complete Kronos row.

German passage transcribed from the scan:

> Aufwärts-Entwicklung in der Öffentlichkeit, besondere Veränderungen im öffentlichen Leben, Entwicklung der öffentlichen Autorität, Öffentlichkeits-Entfaltung des Staates, selbständige Entwicklung in der Öffentlichkeit, wachsende Autorität im öffentlichen Leben

Editorial translation: Upward development in public; special changes in public life; development of public authority; the state's public unfolding; independent development in public; growing authority in public life.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Public development takes the form of a rise, growing authority, or governmental change.
- Psychology: The German ABC passage names independent development; a broader personality account is absent.
- Body/health: Neither entry specifies a bodily or medical meaning.
- Social/relationship: Public authority and the state are the explicit institutional settings.
- Events/manifestations: Government changes, upward development, and increasing public authority are named.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC names independent development and growing public authority; it does not state that independence necessarily produces authority.

## Contradictions

Governmental change and increasing authority are different emphases, not opposing statements. The entries do not say that all changes of government produce a rise.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/Pluto = Kronos`, Kronos specifies public development through rising standing and the development or alteration of authority. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) gives a general rise and governmental change; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) makes public authority and the state explicit. Read together, these support an institutional form of development, with increasing authority among its possibilities rather than a necessary consequence of every public change.
