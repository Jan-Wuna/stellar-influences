## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

General changes in the environment that affect the public or females.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. `42`, one-based PDF p. `20`, complete Moon row.

German passage transcribed from the scan:

> Entwicklung der Frau im öffentlichen Leben, Gemüts-Veränderung in der Öffentlichkeit, Frauen verändern die Welt, öffentliche Entwicklung eines Volkes, Stunde der Veränderung in der Öffentlichkeit, wachsendes Gefühl für die Öffentlichkeit

Editorial translation: Development of women in public life; change of mood in public; women change the world; public development of a people; an hour of change in public; growing feeling for public life.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Environmental and public development involving people and, in the sources' gendered wording, women.
- Psychology: Changes of mood and growing feeling for public life are named.
- Body/health: The entries supply no specific medical or bodily condition.
- Social/relationship: Public development of a people and women's participation in change are explicit source themes.
- Events/manifestations: Changes in the environment and public life are the stated manifestations.
- Conflicts/notes: ABC uses historical gendered examples and an hour-of-change expression; neither establishes a universal gender claim or a timing method.

## Contradictions

Being affected by environmental change and participating in public change are different emphases, not explicit opposites. The historical gendered examples do not establish a universal account of women.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/Pluto = Moon`, Moon specifies public development through its effects on people and their participation in change. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) emphasizes environmental changes affecting the public, while the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) adds public development of a people, changes of mood, and growing feeling for public life. Both also use historical gendered examples involving women; those remain attributed examples rather than a universal gender claim.
