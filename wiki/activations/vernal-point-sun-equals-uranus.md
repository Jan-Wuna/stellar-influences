## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 37 (one-based).

A body becoming suddenly active. High tension of the body. Uproar. Restlessness. Suddenly active. Positive pole of the body.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 23; PDF page: 10 (one-based spread).

Excited people in public, new men in public life, personal impulses for the public, reform in the world of man, day of reform in public life, and technical center in public

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: Sudden personal activity and public reform.
- psychology: Excitement, restlessness and impulses toward renewal.
- body/health: Witte associates bodily tension and sudden activity; his “positive pole” has no explanation here.
- social/relationship: Excited people and new public figures.
- events/manifestations: Public reforms, technical centers and days of renewal.
- conflicts/notes: The bodily-pole term supplies no documented physical mechanism.

## Contradictions

The entries meet in sudden activity. ABC develops public renewal; Witte emphasizes bodily tension. The unexplained “positive pole” is retained as source wording.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

`Vernal Point/Sun = Uranus` connects sudden personal activity with an excited public setting. Witte’s bodily tension and restlessness give this activity an agitated quality; ABC extends it to personal impulses, new public figures and reform in public life. ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry))

Technical centers and days of public renewal are concrete examples of that mobilization. Witte’s “positive pole” remains an unexplained source term and does not establish an electrical or physiological mechanism. ([Witte](#witte-entry), [ABC](#abc-entry))
