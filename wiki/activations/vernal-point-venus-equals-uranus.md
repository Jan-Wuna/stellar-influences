## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Sudden frenzy of love. Sudden events for female persons.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source reference: printed p. `31`, one-based PDF p. `14`, complete Uranus row.

German passage transcribed from the scan:

> Freude über öffentliche Reformen, Friedens-Ereignis in der Öffentlichkeit, harmonische Impulse im öffentlichen Leben, neue Kunst in der Öffentlichkeit, öffentliche Friedens-Reform, öffentliches Kunst-Ereignis, Vorliebe für öffentliche Technik

Editorial translation: Joy over public reforms; a peace event in public; harmonious impulses in public life; new art in public; public peace reform; a public art event; preference for public technology.

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: harmony and affection appear through sudden events, reforms, and fresh public impulses.
- psychology: Witte names a sudden frenzy of love; ABC names pleasure in public reform.
- body/health: neither entry specifies a health condition.
- social/relationship: sudden love is one possibility; public peace and harmony are another setting in ABC.
- events/manifestations: public reform, a peace event, new art, peace reform, a public art event, and preference for public technology are named.
- conflicts/notes: Witte's reference to female persons is a historical source formulation, not a universal gender rule; public and personal events remain different expressions.

## Contradictions

- Sudden love and public reform are different manifestations, without a stated causal connection. Neither establishes a necessary outcome for women.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vernal Point/Venus = Uranus`, Uranus specifies harmony and affection through a sudden impulse or a public change. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) gives sudden love (PDF p. 41), while the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) connects pleasure with reforms, public peace events, new art, and technology (printed p. 31; PDF p. 14). These public and personal manifestations remain alternatives within the entry; Witte's gendered wording does not establish a rule about all women.
