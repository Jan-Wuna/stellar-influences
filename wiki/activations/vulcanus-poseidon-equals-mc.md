## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 315 (one-based).

To be enlightened by a great mind or spirit. Self assurance, Pride. Sense of honor, dignity. To possess a remarkable mental attitude.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 478; PDF page: 238 (one-based spread).

ensouled cultural influence, influence of soul and spirit, powerful spirit soul, my cultural influence, my strong mind, my reasonable influence, my influential idea, my power of insight, my spiritual energy, and strong spirit soul

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: A personally experienced strong mind, insight, and cultural or spiritual influence.
- Psychology: Self-assurance, pride, honor, dignity, and a remarkable mental attitude are explicit.
- Body/health: No medical or bodily-energy claim is supplied.
- Social/relationship: Being enlightened by a great mind and exercising one's own cultural influence are both named.
- Events/manifestations: An influential idea, personal insight, and influence of soul and spirit are examples rather than dated events.
- Conflicts/notes: "Spirit soul" is ABC's spiritual vocabulary; it is not an anatomical or diagnostic term.

## Contradictions

Receiving illumination and possessing one's own strong mind are compatible directions of experience. The entries do not establish that self-assurance proves insight or truth.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Vulcanus/Poseidon = MC`, MC specifies the pair as one's own mental or spiritual strength and sense of dignity. A person may experience illumination through a great mind or recognize their own insight, influential ideas, and cultural effect. The [Witte Entry](#witte-entry) grounds the self-assurance and honor themes (PDF p. 315); the [ABC Entry](#abc-entry) develops the first-person language of insight and influence (printed p. 478; PDF p. 238). This describes a personal experience of significance without proving the correctness of its ideas.
