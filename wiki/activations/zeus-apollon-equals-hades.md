## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

- PDF page: 291 (one-based).

To be faced with the past. Retribution for past evil deeds. Dealing with rags and coal. To study the past.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

- Source page: 453; PDF page: 225 (one-based spread).

Success through effort and deprivation, successful achievements of the past, dangerous spread of fire, dangerous objectives of science, hardship through a major fire, shaping hidden knowledge, and knowledge objectives of the past.

Translation checked against the German scan: printed p. 453 (PDF p. 225).

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Research and enterprise confront the past, deprivation, or dangerous uses of knowledge.
- Psychology: Confronting past deeds is explicit; no psychological diagnosis is established.
- Body/health: No medical condition is specified; fire danger is an event example.
- Social/relationship: Retribution for past evil deeds and success through deprivation are distinct source possibilities.
- Events/manifestations: Studying the past, dealing in rags and coal, hazardous fire spread, and hardship through a major fire.
- Conflicts/notes: Retribution remains Witte's moral language; it does not prove wrongdoing by a person being interpreted.

## Contradictions

The Witte scan reads 'To study the past.' The German scan restores dangerous fire spread, dangerous scientific objectives, and hardship through a major fire; the earlier bland 'their' wording is excluded.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

In `Zeus/Apollon = Hades`, Hades specifies research and practical enterprise through what is past, hidden, deprived, or hazardous. Studying the past and shaping hidden knowledge are constructive possibilities, while dealing with its consequences or pursuing dangerous scientific objectives gives the same field a difficult edge. The ABC entry retains success achieved through deprivation alongside the concrete hazards of spreading fire. ([Witte Entry](#witte-entry), PDF p. 291; [ABC Entry](#abc-entry), printed p. 453, PDF p. 225.)
