## Source Entries

### Alfred Witte, Ludwig Rudolph & Hermann Lefeldt - Rules for Planetary Pictures

#### Witte Entry

Reference: PDF p. 296, Zeus + Poseidon, Asc row; checked against the scan.

To be mentally influenced and impressed by others. To be under the mental guidance of others. Others show their conviction and mental attitude. They are under mental compulsion.

### Udo Rudolph - ABC for Planetary Pictures

#### ABC Entry

Reference: printed p. 458 (PDF p. 228), Zeus + Poseidon, Asc row; checked against the German scan.

Efforts at a cultural place, spiritual leadership of the partner, clear local leadership, clear goals for the surroundings, shaping the culture of the surroundings, reasonable achievements for the surroundings, efforts of reason at a place, and the partner's testimony to truth.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Creative thought and conviction expressed through other people and the immediate surroundings.
- Psychology: Being influenced, impressed, guided, or mentally compelled in Witte's wording.
- Body/health: No bodily or clinical account is given.
- Social/relationship: A partner's leadership and testimony, together with local cultural activity.
- Events/manifestations: Local leadership, cultural shaping of the surroundings, and statements of conviction.
- Conflicts/notes: The entry does not establish whether a particular influence is helpful or coercive.

## Contradictions

The German scan replaces "Addresses of cultural sites" with efforts at a cultural place and resolves "Yemunft" as reason. Mental compulsion and clear local leadership indicate a range of influence rather than a contradiction.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Zeus/Poseidon = Asc`, Asc specifies Zeus/Poseidon through the influence of partners and the immediate social setting on conviction. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) describes being impressed or guided by others, including mental compulsion; the [ABC entry](#abc-entry) locates leadership, cultural effort, and testimony in a partner and a place. Conviction takes shape through relationships and surroundings.

The entries do not settle whether that influence is constructive or coercive, and the language of mental influence is not a clinical diagnosis.
