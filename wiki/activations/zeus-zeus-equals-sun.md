## Source Entries

#### Witte Entry

Source reference: *Rules for Planetary Pictures*, `Zeus + Zeus`, Sun row, PDF page 287 (one-based; visually checked).

The burning body. A man with a goal. A born leader. Creative person. The procreative impulse. The rocket. Man's procreative capacity. To be the leader. The physical fire. Inflammable objects. Exposed to physical compulsion. Obligated to act. Creative day.

## Comparative Schema

- Core meaning: Creative purpose is embodied in a person who leads or acts, with physical fire and compulsion also named.
- Psychology: Having a goal and taking the lead are explicit; the entry also allows the pressure of being obligated to act.
- Body/health: Burning, physical fire, and male procreative capacity are historical source associations, not diagnoses or universal statements about sex or fertility.
- Social/relationship: Leadership and procreation are stated, but the entry supplies no detailed account of partnership or family life.
- Events/manifestations: Creative activity, a creative day, a rocket, inflammable objects, and exposure to physical compulsion are listed.
- Conflicts/notes: Personal initiative and imposed action coexist. The source's male wording is retained as attribution rather than made a restriction on who can express the formula.

## Contradictions

The single source includes both leading and being compelled to act. That difference in agency is retained without treating the phrases as mutually exclusive or claiming independent agreement.

## Derived Synthesis

In `Zeus/Zeus = Sun`, Sun specifies the [pair's purposeful creation](../axes/zeus-zeus.md#pair-summary) through the person who embodies, leads, or carries out the activity. The [Witte entry](#witte-entry) joins a goal, creative personhood, and leadership with a physical urge to act. Its fire, rocket, and inflammable-object examples give this activity a bodily or material expression.

The source also names physical compulsion and obligation, so the formula's agency ranges from taking the lead to having to act. Its procreative and male terminology remains historical source language; it provides no individual fertility, medical, or gender conclusion.
