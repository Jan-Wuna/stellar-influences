Reputation, authority, greatness, hierarchy, direction, governing authorities, state, government agency, chief, dominance, superiority, precedence, importance, something special, independence, and self-confidence

## Comparative Schema

- core meaning: elevated standing, authority, executive power, importance, and mastery, whether held personally or encountered in others ([Witte](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC](#abc-keyword-entry)).
- psychology: independence and self-confidence connect personal self-direction with the wider theme of standing above the ordinary ([Witte](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC](#abc-keyword-entry)).
- body/health: neither standalone entry supplies a bodily or health correspondence.
- social/relationship: rulers, chiefs, managers, heads of families, government authorities, and superiors express a relationship of rank or responsibility ([Witte](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC](#abc-keyword-entry)).
- events/manifestations: executive authority, state or agency affairs, precedence, distinction, and mastery of a skill are explicit forms of elevated standing ([Witte](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC](#abc-keyword-entry)).
- conflicts/notes: Witte explicitly allows others to be superior to the person concerned, so Kronos does not assure personal supremacy. Dominance is named, but the entries do not supply a separate moral judgment about the exercise of authority.

<a id="derived-synthesis"></a>
## Derived Synthesis

Kronos describes elevated standing and the authority or competence attached to it: reputation, executive power, government, management, and mastery. That standing can be institutional, as with state authorities and chiefs; domestic, as with the head of a family; or personal, as with independence and exceptional skill. The ABC keywords also retain distinction, precedence, and self-confidence. ([Witte Factor Entry](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC Keyword Entry](#abc-keyword-entry), printed p. 17.)

The scale is relative. The same standalone evidence permits other people to be superior to the person concerned, so Kronos may describe encountering authority as well as exercising it. Rank, independence, and competence are the supported themes; the entries do not make elevated position an assurance of virtue or a description of its abuse. ([Witte Factor Entry](#witte-factor-entry); [ABC Keyword Entry](#abc-keyword-entry).)
